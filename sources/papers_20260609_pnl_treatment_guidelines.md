# Pure Neuritic Leprosy: Treatment Guidelines & Management Papers

## 1. Jardim MR, Illarramendi X, Nascimento OJM, Nery JAC, Sales AM, Sampaio EP, Sarno EN. Pure neural leprosy: steroids prevent neuropathy progression.
- **Journal:** Arquivos de Neuro-Psiquiatria
- **Year:** 2007
- **DOI:** 10.1590/s0004-282x2007000600009
- **PMID:** 18094856
- **Key Findings:** MDT (rifampicin, dapsone, clofazimine) treats infection but is insufficient for preventing nerve damage. Case-series of 24 PNL patients (88% male) showing combined use of steroids and MDT prevents nerve damage progression. Key evidence for adjunctive corticosteroid use in PNL.

## 2. Pinheiro LCS, Borges JC, Campos VR, Dantas LC. An Overview of Treatment Guidelines and Methods of Synthesis of Drugs Used in Leprosy Chemotherapy.
- **Journal:** Mini Reviews in Medicinal Chemistry
- **Year:** 2023
- **DOI:** 10.2174/1389557522666220415233627
- **PMID:** 35430972
- **Key Findings:** Comprehensive review of leprosy chemotherapy. MDT is effective but high-dose long-duration treatment with adverse effects leads to abandonment. Antimicrobial resistance remains a challenge. Reviews WHO treatment guidelines and drug synthesis methods.

## 3. Jain A, Sharma S, Gupta V, Khaitan A. Programmatic Addition of Clofazimine to Multidrug Therapy for Paucibacillary Leprosy in India.
- **Journal:** The American Journal of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene
- **Year:** 2025 (Sep)
- **DOI:** 10.4269/ajtmh.25-0131
- **PMID:** 40555205
- **Key Findings:** Discusses India's 2025 policy change adding clofazimine to paucibacillary MDT regimen. Relevant to PNL treatment since PNL is classified as paucibacillary in most systems. Questions one-size-fits-all approach.

## 4. Pitta IJR, Hacker MA, Vital RT, Andrade LR, Spitz CN, Sales AM, et al. Leprosy Reactions and Neuropathic Pain in Pure Neural Leprosy.
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Medicine
- **Year:** 2022
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fmed.2022.1059448
- **PMID:** 35402428
- **Key Findings:** Leprosy reactions can occur before, during, or after MDT and are a major cause of nerve damage. Neuritis (inflammatory process causing nerve function impairment) can be the sole manifestation of a leprosy reaction in PNL. Important for management of reactions in PNL.

## 5. Dos Santos DF, Mendonça MR, Antunes DE, Sabino EFP, Pereira RC, Goulart LR, Goulart IMB. Revisiting primary neural leprosy: Clinical, serological, molecular, and neurophysiological aspects.
- **Journal:** PLoS Neglected Tropical Diseases
- **Year:** 2017
- **DOI:** 10.1371/journal.pntd.0005965
- **PMID:** 29176796
- **Key Findings:** Comprehensive study of primary neural leprosy. Clinical diagnosis is challenging since no skin lesions and slit skin smear is negative. Addresses controversial concepts regarding primary-neural vs pure-neural leprosy classification.

## 6. de Freitas MRG, Said G. Leprous neuropathy.
- **Journal:** Handbook of Clinical Neurology
- **Year:** 2013
- **DOI:** 10.1016/B978-0-444-52902-2.00028-X
- **PMID:** 23931798
- **Key Findings:** Authoritative chapter on leprous neuropathy. Clinical and pathological manifestations determined by host immune resistance. Failure of early detection leads to severe disability. Reviews treatment approaches including MDT and steroid use.

## 7. Bhoi SK, Naik S, Purkait S. Pure Neuritic Leprosy with Bilateral Foot Drop and Central Nervous Involvement.
- **Journal:** Neurology India
- **Year:** 2021 (Sep-Oct)
- **DOI:** 10.4103/0028-3886.329620
- **PMID:** 34747810
- **Key Findings:** Demonstrates CNS involvement in PNL using neurophysiological tests and MRI. Has implications for treatment — PNL may not be limited to peripheral nerves, suggesting need for more comprehensive evaluation and management.

## 8. Bhoi SK, Lahre Y, Jha M, Naik S, Purkait S, Samal P, et al. From periphery to center, untold story of pure neuritic leprosy: an electrophysiological study.
- **Journal:** Acta Neurologica Belgica
- **Year:** 2024 (Jun)
- **DOI:** 10.1007/s13760-024-02503-2
- **PMID:** 38438636
- **Key Findings:** Large prospective study showing CNS involvement in PNL through VEP, SSEP, and BAEP abnormalities. Challenges the assumption that PNL is purely a peripheral nerve disease.

## 9. Bhoi SK, Naik S, Lahre Y, Jha M, Purkait S, Samal P, et al. Involvement Beyond Peripheral Nerves in Pure Neuritic Leprosy: An MR Imaging Study.
- **Journal:** AJNR (American Journal of Neuroradiology)
- **Year:** 2025 (Apr)
- **DOI:** 10.3174/ajnr.A8562
- **PMID:** 40113255
- **Key Findings:** Prospective MR imaging study showing CNS changes in PNL patients. Further evidence that PNL may involve the nervous system beyond peripheral nerves.

## 10. Kolleri JJ, Sasidharanpillai S, Vadakkayil B, Chathoth AT. A 10-year Retrospective Descriptive Study on Pure Neuritic Leprosy from a Tertiary Referral Centre.
- **Journal:** Indian Dermatology Online Journal
- **Year:** 2019 (Jan-Feb)
- **DOI:** 10.4103/idoj.IDOJ_118_18
- **PMID:** 30788282
- **Key Findings:** 10-year retrospective data from tertiary center providing epidemiological and clinical patterns of PNL, informing treatment decisions.

### WHO Guidelines Note:
The WHO 2024/2025 leprosy treatment guidelines recommend:
- **Multidrug therapy (MDT):** Standard regimen of rifampicin + dapsone for paucibacillary leprosy (6 months), rifampicin + dapsone + clofazimine for multibacillary leprosy (12 months)
- **PNL classification:** WHO classifies PNL as paucibacillary (PB) for treatment purposes, though many experts argue for multibacillary (MB) treatment due to higher relapse risk
- **Corticosteroids:** Adjunctive prednisolone recommended for nerve function impairment (starting 40-60 mg/day, tapering over 12-24 weeks)
- India has recently added clofazimine to the PB regimen (2025 policy change), which would affect PNL treatment
