# Research Notes — Mycoplasma genitalium Case

**Case Title:** The Persistent Burn
**Slug:** mycoplasma-genitalium
**Target Date:** 2026-05-19
**Audience:** ID Fellow

## Key Clinical Facts

### Microbiology
- Smallest known self-replicating free-living bacterium (200–300 nm diameter)
- Class Mollicutes (no cell wall) — all beta-lactams intrinsically ineffective
- Reduced genome (~580 kbp, <500 proteins)
- Terminal attachment organelle mediates adherence to urethral epithelium and microcolony formation
- Requires cholesterol for membrane biosynthesis (cannot synthesize own)

### Epidemiology
- Accounts for 15–25% of non-gonococcal urethritis (NGU) in men
- Associated with cervicitis, PID, and possibly tubal factor infertility in women
- Prevalence: 1–2% in general population, higher in STI clinic attendees
- Most common cause of persistent/recurrent urethritis after standard GC/CT therapy

### Clinical Presentation
- Men: dysuria, mucopurulent discharge (often thinner than GC)
- Women: may be asymptomatic or present with cervicitis, discharge, dyspareunia
- Incubation period: 7–21 days (longer than GC)
- Often presents as "persistent urethritis" after standard NGU treatment

### Diagnosis
- FDA-cleared test: Aptima (Hologic) — transcription-mediated amplification (TMA)
- Targets 16S ribosomal RNA (not genomic DNA)
- TMA: isothermal amplification using reverse transcriptase + T7 RNA polymerase
- rRNA present at 10^3–10^4 copies/organism → high sensitivity
- Organism burden often as low as 10–1000 copies/swab
- Culture: possible on SP4 agar with cholesterol, but requires weeks of incubation, barely visible granular colonies — not clinically practical
- No FDA-cleared serologic tests; cross-reactivity with M. pneumoniae
- No rapid antigen tests

### Treatment
- 2021 CDC STI Treatment Guidelines key recommendations:
  - **Single-dose azithromycin (1g): explicitly contraindicated** due to macrolide resistance >50%
  - When resistance testing unavailable: **doxycycline 100mg BID x 7d → moxifloxacin 400mg daily x 7d**
  - When macrolide susceptible confirmed: doxycycline 100mg BID x 7d → azithromycin 500mg day 1 + 250mg days 2–5 (2.5g total)
  - Test of cure: repeat NAAT at 3–4 weeks post-treatment
  - All sexual partners within 60 days should be evaluated and treated

### Resistance
- Macrolide resistance: >50% in US, driven by 23S rRNA mutations (A2058G, A2059G)
- Fluoroquinolone resistance: emerging, ~5–15% in US (parC mutations)
- Doxycycline resistance: not described, but limited efficacy (~30–40% cure as monotherapy)
- Mechanism of macrolide resistance: point mutations in 23S rRNA gene prevent macrolide binding to 50S ribosomal subunit

## Question Design Notes

### Q1 — Diagnosis
- Correct answer: M. genitalium (cell wall-free, doxycycline only 30-40% curative)
- T. vaginalis distractor: plausible (not treated by ceftriaxone/doxy), but less common etiology
- Other distractors: ceftriaxone-resistant GC, persistent CT, HSV urethritis

### Q2 — Test Mechanism
- Correct answer: TMA targeting 16S rRNA (isothermal, RNA-based, not PCR)
- Distractor: culture on SP4 agar (technically possible but weeks for growth)
- Distractor: serology (cross-reactive, no clinical utility)
- Distractor: Gram stain (no cell wall, organisms below resolution)
- Distractor: lateral flow antigen (does not exist)

### Q3 — Treatment
- Correct answer: doxycycline x7d → moxifloxacin x7d (sequential, CDC-recommended)
- Key teaching: azithromycin single dose is contraindicated
- Key teaching: doxycycline pretreatment reduces bacterial load ~2.6 log
