# Rotavirus HistorID source notes

## Sources checked

1. Bishop RF, Davidson GP, Holmes IH, Ruck BJ. Virus particles in epithelial cells of duodenal mucosa from children with acute non-bacterial gastroenteritis.
   URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0140673673928675
   Key details: primary discovery paper from 1973. Bishop and colleagues at the Royal Children's Hospital and University of Melbourne identified viral particles in duodenal epithelial cells of children with acute gastroenteritis. This is the first visualization of human rotavirus.

2. Flewett TH, Bryden AS, Davies H, Woode GN, Bridger JC, Derrick JM. Relation between viruses from acute gastroenteritis of children and newborn calves.
   URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0140673674916316
   Key details: the 1974 Lancet paper where Flewett and colleagues explicitly suggested the name rotavirus on the basis of the pathogen's wheel-like shape under electron microscopy.

3. CDC Etymologia: Rotavirus
   URL: https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/17/8/et-1708_article
   Key details: confirms rotavirus comes from Latin rota meaning wheel. States Flewett et al. suggested the name in 1974 and the ICTV approved it four years later. Good concise etymology source.

4. Bishop R. Discovery of rotavirus: Implications for child health.
   URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19799704/
   Key details: Ruth Bishop's own 2009 historical review. Notes that before 1973 no infectious agents could be identified in about 80% of children admitted with severe dehydrating diarrhea. Useful for clinical context and modern relevance.

5. ICTV Rotavirus genus page
   URL: https://ictv.global/report/chapter/sedoreoviridae/sedoreoviridae/rotavirus
   Key details: authoritative taxonomy source. Confirms wheel-like appearance gives the genus its name from Latin rota. Good for morphological and taxonomic precision.

6. Wikimedia Commons, Rotavirus TEM
   URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rotavirus_TEM_B82-0337_lores.jpg
   Key details: CDC-origin transmission electron micrograph of rotavirus particles. Public domain via U.S. government/CDC. Shows the classic wheel-like morphology that gave the virus its name.

## Drafting notes

- The strongest hook is the gap itself: before 1973, most severe childhood diarrhea had no identified cause.
- Bishop's team used duodenal biopsies and electron microscopy to see the virus directly in human tissue.
- Flewett proposed the name rotavirus based on the wheel-like appearance.
- Keep the scientific words in place: rotavirus, rota, electron microscopy, duodenal epithelial cells.
- The story is tight: discovery (1973), naming (1974), formal taxonomy (1978).
