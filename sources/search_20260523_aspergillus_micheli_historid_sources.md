# Aspergillus / Pier Antonio Micheli etymology HistorID source notes

## Core narrative

Pier Antonio Micheli (1679-1737), a Catholic priest and botanist in Florence, Director of the Botanical Garden of Florence (Giardino dei Semplici), published *Nova Plantarum Genera* in 1729. Looking at a mold under his microscope, he saw that the conidial head — a swollen vesicle with radiating chains of spores — resembled an aspergillum, the metal wand used by Catholic priests to sprinkle holy water during Mass. He named the genus *Aspergillus*, Latin for "little sprinkler" (from *aspergere*, "to sprinkle"). Micheli was also the first to demonstrate experimentally that fungi reproduce by spores.

## Etymology trail

- Latin: *aspergere* = to sprinkle, to scatter, to strew
- Latin: *aspergillum* = sprinkler (the liturgical instrument for holy water)
- New Latin: *Aspergillus* = little sprinkler (diminutive form)
- The Asperges rite: Catholic Mass opening rite where the priest sprinkles holy water on the congregation, accompanied by the antiphon "Asperges me, Domine, hyssopo et mundabor" (Psalm 51:7)

## Sources

1. Micheli PA. Nova Plantarum Genera juxta Tournefortii methodum disposita. Florence: Typis Bernardi Paperinii; 1729.
   Key details: The original publication. Described ~1,900 species including ~900 new to science. Named Aspergillus, Botrytis, Mucor, and other fungal genera still in use. Includes his spore germination experiments on melon and quince slices.

2. Ainsworth GC. Introduction to the History of Mycology. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press; 1976. pp. 33-45.
   Key details: The standard history of mycology. Covers Micheli's contributions in detail, including his spore experiments, his microscopy techniques, and the context of early 18th-century botanical science.

3. Ainsworth GC, Bisby GR. Ainsworth and Bisby's Dictionary of the Fungi. 10th ed. Wallingford: CAB International; 2008.
   Key details: Entry for Aspergillus documents the etymological origin: Latin *aspergillum* = holy water sprinkler, diminutive *Aspergillus*. Confirms Micheli as the author of the genus name in 1729.

4. Hawksworth DL. Pier Antonio Micheli (1679-1737): the founder of mycology. Mycologist. 1991;5(1):6-10.
   Key details: Short biography focused on Micheli's scientific contributions. Confirms his role as founder of mycology, his spore germination experiments, and the etymology of Aspergillus.

## Additional context for drafting

### Micheli biography
- Born: 1679, Florence, Italy
- Died: 1737, Florence
- Catholic priest (ordained)
- Director of the Botanical Garden of Florence (Giardino dei Semplici) from 1706 to 1737
- Member of the Accademia dei Lincei (one of the first scientific societies)
- Corresponded with leading botanists across Europe
- His herbarium and manuscripts are preserved at the University of Florence

### Aspergillus today
- ~250 species in the genus
- A. fumigatus: most common airborne fungal pathogen, invasive aspergillosis, mortality >50%
- A. flavus and A. parasiticus: aflatoxin producers, heptocellular carcinoma
- A. terreus: intrinsically resistant to amphotericin B
- A. niger: otomycosis, common lab contaminant
- Allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis (ABPA)
- Galactomannan: serum and BAL biomarker
- Voriconazole: first-line treatment
- Triazole resistance: CYP51A mutations, environmental fungicide exposure

### Micheli's spore experiments
- Placed fungal spores on freshly cut slices of melon and quince
- Observed under his compound microscope that spores germinated and produced hyphae
- New fungal growth of the same species appeared
- Repeated with multiple species
- Concluded: fungi reproduce by spores, not spontaneous generation
- This was a revolutionary finding for the period
