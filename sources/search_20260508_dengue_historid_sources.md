# Dengue HistorID source notes

## Sources checked

1. Online Etymology Dictionary, `dengue`
   URL: https://www.etymonline.com/word/dengue
   Key details: English `dengue` is recorded from 1828, from West Indian Spanish. The entry notes a possible African source, perhaps Swahili `dinga`, and says the final form may have been influenced by Spanish `dengue`, with the clinical image of a stiff walk in the background.

2. Encyclopaedia Britannica, `dengue`
   URL: https://www.britannica.com/science/dengue
   Key details: summarizes early recognized epidemics in Cairo and Batavia in 1779 and Philadelphia in 1780, and notes Benjamin Rush's classic clinical description. Also useful for the older names `breakbone fever` and `dandy fever`.

3. Halstead SB. Dengue. `Lancet`. 2007;370(9599):1644-1652.
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(07)61687-0
   Key details: broad review used for modern historical and clinical framing.

4. World Health Organization. `Dengue: Guidelines for Diagnosis, Treatment, Prevention and Control`. 2009.
   URL: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK143161/
   Key details: clear explanation of the febrile, critical, and recovery phases, including the 24- to 48-hour plasma leakage window around defervescence.

5. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. About dengue.
   URL: https://www.cdc.gov/dengue/about/index.html
   Key details: current clinical overview and the CDC page used as the institutional source for the hero image showing a female adult `Aedes aegypti` feeding on a human.

## Drafting notes

- The etymology is interesting precisely because it is not clean. The final copy should say that plainly.
- The strongest modern teaching pearl is the mismatch between the old pain-centered name and the actual high-risk window around defervescence.
- The hero image does not have to be historical if it is direct, institutional, and clearly tied to dengue transmission.
