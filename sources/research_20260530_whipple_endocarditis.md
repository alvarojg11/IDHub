## Key References

### 1. Fenollar F, et al. Emerg Infect Dis. 2013;19(11):1721-30.
- 28 cases of T. whipplei endocarditis diagnosed in Marseille
- Disease in men 40-80, 75% arthralgia, 39% fever, 32% prior valvular disease
- Duke criteria insensitive before valve analysis
- Treatment: doxycycline + hydroxychloroquine for at least 12 months
- DOI: 10.3201/eid1911.121356

### 2. McGee M, et al. Open Forum Infect Dis. 2018;6(1):ofy330.
- Literature review: 44 publications, 169 patients
- Mean age 57.1, 85% male
- Articular involvement 52%, heart failure 41%
- Aortic valve most common, mortality ~24%
- DOI: 10.1093/ofid/ofy330

### 3. Dolmans RA, et al. Clin Microbiol Rev. 2017;30(2):529-555.
- Comprehensive review: epidemiology, pathogenesis, diagnosis, treatment
- PAS staining: poor specificity and sensitivity alone
- Molecular detection (PCR) greatly improved diagnosis
- DOI: 10.1128/CMR.00033-16

### 4. Boumaza A, et al. Lancet Infect Dis. 2022;22(10):e280-e291.
- Most up-to-date comprehensive review
- T. whipplei ubiquitous in environment, Whipple's disease very rare
- Host immune defect suspected in those who cannot clear
- DOI: 10.1016/S1473-3099(22)00128-1

### 5. Marth T, et al. Lancet Infect Dis. 2016;16(3):e13-e22.
- Pathogenesis, treatment strategies
- Controlled trials of different approaches

## Key Clinical Facts for Case Writing

- **Organism**: Tropheryma whipplei, gram-positive actinomycete
- **Culture**: Cannot be cultured on routine media; first cultured in 2000 in HEL cell lines
- **Histopathology**: PAS-positive, diastase-resistant foamy macrophages = hallmark
- **Molecular diagnosis**: 16S rRNA PCR from valve tissue is gold standard
- **Demographics**: 85% male, age 40-80
- **Arthralgias**: Present in 52-75%, precede cardiac/GI manifestations by years (median 6 years)
- **GI symptoms**: May be absent in isolated endocarditis form
- **Valve**: Aortic valve most commonly involved
- **Blood cultures**: Always negative (obligate intracellular pathogen)
- **Treatment**: 
  - Induction: IV ceftriaxone or meropenem x 2 weeks
  - Maintenance: Doxycycline + hydroxychloroquine x 12+ months
  - Mechanism: Hydroxychloroquine alkalinizes phagolysosome (pH-dependent doxycycline activity)
  - Historical alternative: TMP-SMX (but resistance and relapse issues)
- **Prognosis**: Fatal without treatment; ~24% mortality even with treatment
- **Duke criteria**: Frequently insensitive for T. whipplei endocarditis
- **Geographic clustering**: Eastern-central France, Switzerland, Germany
