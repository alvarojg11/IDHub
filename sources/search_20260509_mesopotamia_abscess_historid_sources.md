# Mesopotamia abscess HistorID source notes

## Sources checked

1. Fordham Ancient History Sourcebook, `Code of Hammurabi`
   URL: https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/ancient/hamcode.asp
   Key details: L. W. King translation includes the physician laws. Law 215 rewards successful incision with an operating knife. Law 218 states that if the physician makes a large incision and kills the patient, or opens the tumor and destroys the eye, "his hands shall be cut off." Laws 219-220 shift penalties for enslaved patients to replacement or financial compensation.

2. Retief FP, Cilliers L. `Mesopotamian medicine`. `S Afr Med J`. 2007;97(1):27-30.
   URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17378276/
   Key details: helpful overview of Mesopotamian medical culture, including the mix of divination, exorcism, and practical treatment, plus the context of codified medical practice.

3. Encyclopaedia Britannica, `history of medicine`
   URL: https://www.britannica.com/science/history-of-medicine
   Key details: directly summarizes the Hammurabi example as opening an abscess and notes that failure could cost the doctor's hands. Useful bridge for the ID angle because it frames the procedure in recognizable clinical language.

4. Encyclopaedia Britannica, `Code of Hammurabi`
   URL: https://www.britannica.com/topic/Code-of-Hammurabi
   Key details: general legal and historical context for the code.

5. Louvre Museum collections, `Code de Hammurabi`, SB 8
   URL: https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010174436
   Key details: direct institutional record for the stele, including date, material, dimensions, place of discovery, and current display location.

6. Wikimedia Commons, `P1050763 Louvre code Hammurabi face rwk-gradient.jpg`
   URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:P1050763_Louvre_code_Hammurabi_face_rwk-gradient.jpg
   Key details: square front-view photograph of the stele in the Louvre. Photographer: Mbzt. License: CC BY 3.0. Good candidate for HistorID hero image when direct museum image licensing is not clearly reusable.

## Drafting notes

- Keep the tone balanced: the brutality of the punishment is the hook, but the piece should still land on source control rather than ancient shock value alone.
- Avoid overstating microbiologic understanding. The point is not that Babylonians understood infection in modern terms, but that they already faced dangerous localized disease that sometimes required incision.
- It is worth acknowledging the translation tension between `abscess` in modern summaries and `tumor (over the eye)` in King's translation.
