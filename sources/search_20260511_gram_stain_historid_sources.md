# Gram stain HistorID source notes

## Sources checked

1. Encyclopaedia Britannica, `Gram stain`
   URL: https://www.britannica.com/science/Gram-stain
   Key details: confirms that the stain was devised by Hans Christian Gram in 1884 and summarizes the crystal violet, iodine, alcohol/acetone, and counterstain logic, along with the later interpretation of Gram-positive and Gram-negative cell wall differences.

2. Archived `Pioneers in Medical Laboratory Science` historical note and translated excerpt from Gram's 1884 paper
   URL: https://web.archive.org/web/20160810225816/http://www.hoslink.com/pathology/lab_general/history2.htm#gram
   Key details: this is the most useful narrative source for the original context. It states that Gram was working with Friedländer in Berlin, trying to demonstrate cocci in tissue sections of lungs from people who had died of pneumonia. It quotes the key practical point that nuclei and other tissue elements remained unstained while cocci stained strongly, making them much easier to find in exudative tissue. It also preserves Gram's modest closing line that his results were brief and had many gaps, and that he hoped the method would be useful in other hands.

3. Wikimedia Commons, `Hans Christian Gram portrait by Hansen & Weller`
   URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hans_Christian_Gram_portrait_by_Hansen_%26_Weller.jpg
   Key details: public-domain portrait suitable for hero image. Original source traces to the Royal Danish Library image archive.

## Drafting notes

- The best hook is not the positive/negative split by itself. The stronger story is that Gram first needed a way to make pneumonia cocci visible in tissue at all.
- Keep Gram's own modesty. The line about the work being brief and full of gaps makes the story more alive.
- Do not oversell the 1884 moment as if the mature Gram-positive/Gram-negative framework arrived fully formed on day one. Better to show the method widening in importance after its first practical use.
