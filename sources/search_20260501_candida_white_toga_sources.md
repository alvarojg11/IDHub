# Candida and the White Toga Sources

Date: 2026-05-01

## Etymology

Source: Online Etymology Dictionary, `candidate`.

URL: https://www.etymonline.com/word/candidate

Key evidence: `candidate` comes from Latin `candidatus`, originally "white-robed," from `candidare`, "to make white or bright," from `candidus`, from `candere`, "to shine." The entry also notes that Roman office-seekers wore a gleaming white `toga candida`, likely whitened with powdered chalk.

## Candida Taxonomy

Source: Crossref metadata for Mackinnon JE, Artagaveytia-Allende RC. The So-Called Genus Candida Berkhout, 1923. Journal of Bacteriology. 1945;49(4):317-334. DOI: 10.1128/jb.49.4.317-334.1945.

URL: https://doi.org/10.1128/jb.49.4.317-334.1945

Key evidence: Article title and references confirm the Berkhout 1923 genus context and cite Berkhout CM. 1923. `Les genres Monilia Oidium Oospora et Torula`, thesis, Utrecht University. Crossref references also document earlier naming/taxonomy terms including `Oidium albicans`, `Monilia albicans`, and later `Candida albicans` literature.

## Modern Clinical Candida Context

Source: NCBI Bookshelf chapter, Jenkinson HF, Douglas LJ. Interactions between Candida Species and Bacteria in Mixed Infections. In: Polymicrobial Diseases. ASM Press; 2002.

URL: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK2486/

Key evidence: `Candida albicans` is described as an opportunistic fungal pathogen and commensal found in the human digestive tract, associated with mucosal disease, biofilms, prosthetic material, and systemic infections.
