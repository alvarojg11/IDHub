# Hepatitis naming HistorID source notes

## Sources checked

1. MacCallum FO. Homologous serum jaundice. Lancet. 1947;250(6480):691-692.
   URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(47)91114-8
   Key details: The paper where MacCallum proposed replacing "infectious hepatitis" and "serum hepatitis" with Hepatitis A and Hepatitis B. He argued that the old names were misleading because both viruses could transmit via both routes. The letters were deliberately neutral.

2. Alter HJ, Purcell RH, Holland PV, Feinstone SM, Morrow AG, Moritsugu Y. Clinical and serological analysis of transfusion-associated hepatitis. Lancet. 1975;2(7940):838-841.
   URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(75)90234-2
   Key details: The paper where Alter and colleagues demonstrated that most post-transfusion hepatitis was caused by neither A nor B, coining the term "non-A, non-B hepatitis" (NANBH). This placeholder name lasted 14 years until HCV was cloned.

3. Choo QL, Kuo G, Weiner AJ, Overby LR, Bradley DW, Houghton M. Isolation of a cDNA clone derived from a blood-borne non-A, non-B viral hepatitis genome. Science. 1989;244(4902):359-362.
   URL: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.2523562
   Key details: The landmark paper from Chiron Corporation that cloned hepatitis C virus using blind immunoscreening of cDNA from infected chimpanzee plasma. This ended the NANBH era. Alter, Houghton, and Rice won the 2020 Nobel Prize.

4. Balayan MS, Andjaparidze AG, Savinskaya SS, et al. Evidence for a virus in non-A, non-B hepatitis transmitted via the fecal-oral route. Intervirology. 1983;20(1):23-31.
   URL: https://doi.org/10.1159/000149370
   Key details: Discovery of hepatitis E virus. Balayan self-experimented by drinking pooled stool extract from infected soldiers and developed acute hepatitis, then detected the virus in his own stool by immune electron microscopy.

5. Rizzetto M, Canese MG, Arico S, et al. Immunofluorescence detection of new antigen-antibody system (delta/anti-delta) associated to hepatitis B virus in liver and in serum of HBsAg carriers. Gut. 1977;18(12):997-1003.
   URL: https://doi.org/10.1136/gut.18.12.997
   Key details: Discovery of the delta antigen (hepatitis D) in liver biopsies from HBV carriers in Turin. Rizzetto initially thought it was a new HBV antigen; it was later proven to be a separate defective virus.

6. Wikimedia Commons: Harvey Alter portrait
   URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Harvey_Alter_in_2020_(cropped).jpg
   Key details: NIH photo of Dr. Harvey Alter holding his Nobel Prize medal, December 8, 2020. Public domain (NIH/US federal government work). Alter coined "non-A, non-B hepatitis" in 1975.

## Drafting notes

- The strongest hook is the "non-A, non-B" era — 14 years of calling a disease by what it wasn't.
- MacCallum's 1947 choice of letters was deliberately neutral. He couldn't have known it would lock in an alphabet for 75+ years.
- Alter's naming of NANBH was an honest placeholder. When the virus was cloned in 1989, the letter C was waiting.
- The Balayan self-experiment is a striking detail but shouldn't overshadow the main story.
- F and G are ghosts — they show the alphabet outran the diseases.
- The lesson: naming things by what they aren't (rather than by what we think they are) can be more scientifically durable.
- Keep the tone conversational. The story is about improvisation, not design.
