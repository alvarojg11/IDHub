### Key Clinical Data Points

**Taxonomy (S. bovis/equinus group):**
- S. gallolyticus subsp. gallolyticus (formerly S. bovis biotype I) — STRONGEST CRC association
- S. gallolyticus subsp. pasteurianus
- S. gallolyticus subsp. macedonicus
- S. infantarius subsp. infantarius (formerly S. bovis II/1)
- S. infantarius subsp. coli (formerly S. bovis II/2)
- S. alactolyticus, S. equinus, S. lutetiensis

**Microbiology:**
- Gram-positive cocci in pairs and short chains
- Alpha-hemolytic (some strains gamma)
- Catalase-negative
- Bile esculin-positive (KEY distinguishing feature from viridans streptococci)
- PYR-negative (distinguishes from Enterococcus, which is PYR-positive)
- Optochin-resistant (distinguishes from S. pneumoniae)
- Does not grow in 6.5% NaCl (distinguishes from Enterococcus)

**CRC Association:**
- Prevalence: 47-71% of patients with S. gallolyticus subsp. gallolyticus bacteremia have colorectal neoplasia
- Association stronger for subsp. gallolyticus than other subspecies
- First recognized in the 1950s

**Mechanism:**
- Pilus-mediated adhesion: Pil1 pilus (Gallo2179, Gallo2178), Pil2, Pil3
- Bind tumor-associated glycans and mucins
- Tumor provides niche, disrupted barrier allows translocation
- Not carcinogenic — exploits pre-existing neoplastic tissue

**Treatment:**
- Penicillin G or ceftriaxone (4-6 weeks for endocarditis)
- Nearly universal beta-lactam susceptibility
- Vancomycin for severe beta-lactam allergy
- Follows AHA/IDSA guidelines for streptococcal endocarditis
