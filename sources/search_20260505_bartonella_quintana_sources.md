## Historical trench fever frame

Source: Anstead GM. The centenary of the discovery of trench fever, an emerging infectious disease of World War 1. Lancet Infect Dis. 2016;16(8):e164-e172.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S1473-3099(16)30003-2

Key evidence used:

- Trench fever was a major disabling infection of World War I.
- The syndrome was strongly associated with body lice and filthy wartime living conditions.
- Its military importance came from how many soldiers it incapacitated, not from the kind of explosive mortality seen with some other epidemic diseases.

## Meaning of quintan / quintana

Source: Quintan fever. Farlex Partner Medical Dictionary and related medical dictionary entries.

URL: https://medical-dictionary.thefreedictionary.com/quintan+fever

Key evidence used:

- `quintan` refers to a fever recurring every five days by older inclusive counting.
- `quintan fever` is listed as an older synonym for trench fever or five-day fever.

## Modern organism review

Source: Foucault C, Brouqui P, Raoult D. Bartonella quintana characteristics and clinical management. Emerg Infect Dis. 2006;12(2):217-223.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.3201/eid1202.050874

Key evidence used:

- `B. quintana` is restricted to human hosts and louse vectors.
- The organism was first characterized as the agent of trench fever.
- It is now recognized as a reemerging pathogen among homeless populations in the United States and Europe.
- The clinical spectrum includes chronic bacteremia, endocarditis, and bacillary angiomatosis.

Local metadata file: `sources/pubmed_20260505_bquintana_modern_review.json`.

Source: Raoult D, Roux V. The body louse as a vector of reemerging human diseases. Clin Infect Dis. 1999;29(4):888-911.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1086/520454

Key evidence used:

- Body lice live and multiply in clothing rather than staying on skin full-time.
- Louse infestation is tied to cold weather and lack of hygiene.
- Among the diseases spread by body lice, `B. quintana` now appears in bacteremia, trench fever, endocarditis, and chronic lymphadenopathy among people experiencing homelessness.

## Modern re-emergence in homelessness medicine

Source: Shepard Z, Vargas Barahona L, Montalbano G, Rowan SE, Franco-Paredes C, Madinger N. Bartonella quintana Infection in People Experiencing Homelessness in the Denver Metropolitan Area. J Infect Dis. 2022;226(Suppl 3):S315-S321.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/infdis/jiac238

Key evidence used:

- `B. quintana` remains underdiagnosed because of nonspecific presentations.
- The organism remains tied to barriers in hygiene and housing rather than to war alone.

Source: Keller M, Agladze M, Kupferman T, et al. Bartonella quintana Endocarditis in Persons Experiencing Homelessness, New York, New York, USA, 2020-2023. Emerg Infect Dis. 2024;30(12):2494-2501.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.3201/eid3012.240433

Key evidence used:

- `B. quintana` causes left-sided culture-negative endocarditis in modern urban settings.
- Recent New York cases in people experiencing homelessness included heart failure, renal failure, embolic stroke, valve replacement, and death.
- Housing status and history of possible body-louse exposure are clinically useful clues.

## Wider time horizon

Source: Mai BHA, Barbieri R, Chenal T, et al. Five millennia of Bartonella quintana bacteraemia. PLoS One. 2020;15(11):e0239526.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0239526

Key evidence used:

- `B. quintana` long predates World War I.
- Ancient DNA evidence makes it useful to say that trench fever made the organism famous, but did not create it.

## Verified details used

- `quintan fever` should be explained as the older five-day recurrence label, not over-explained as a linguistics aside.
- The strongest historical claim is that trench fever disabled huge numbers of soldiers in World War I.
- The vector point should emphasize that body lice live in clothing seams, which makes the story feel concrete.
- The modern landing should be chronic bacteremia and culture-negative endocarditis, especially in people facing barriers to hygiene and housing.
- The article should explicitly say that the organism left the trenches but not the conditions that favor it.
