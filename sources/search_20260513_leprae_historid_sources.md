# Leprae HistorID source notes

## Sources checked

1. LPSN, `Mycobacterium leprae`
   URL: https://lpsn.dsmz.de/species/mycobacterium-leprae
   Key details: strongest naming source. Explicitly gives the etymology as `L. fem. n. lepra, leprosy; L. gen. fem. n. leprae, of leprosy.` Also notes the older basonym `Bacillus leprae` and later recombination into `Mycobacterium leprae`.

2. Online Etymology Dictionary, `leper`
   URL: https://www.etymonline.com/word/leper
   Key details: traces `lepra` to Greek `lepra` / `lepros`, with the sense of scaly, scabby, rough, related to peeling and scales. Also notes that old English usage could shift between disease label and person label.

3. Online Etymology Dictionary, `leprosy`
   URL: https://www.etymonline.com/word/leprosy
   Key details: confirms the later English disease noun and notes the 20th-century use of `Hansen's disease`.

4. CDC, `About Leprosy (Hansen's Disease)`
   URL: https://www.cdc.gov/leprosy/about/index.html
   Key details: modern clinical framing, low transmissibility, curability, persistence of stigma, and the useful correction that fingers and toes do not simply “fall off.”

5. Singh P, Cole ST. `Mycobacterium leprae: genes, pseudogenes and genetic diversity.` `Future Microbiol`. 2011;6(1):57-71.
   URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3076554/
   Key details: excellent modern bridge source. States that Hansen's 1873 discovery made `M. leprae` the first bacterial pathogen associated with a human disease, and gives strong material on genomic conservation, reductive evolution, and phylogeography through human migration.

6. Encyclopaedia Britannica, `Leprosy`
   URL: https://www.britannica.com/science/leprosy
   Key details: strong language on the disproportion between historic dread and actual transmissibility; good for the stigma section and for modern myth correction.

7. CDC Public Health Image Library, PHIL #2123
   URL: https://phil.cdc.gov/Details.aspx?pid=2123
   Key details: public-domain photomicrograph of a patient's lepromatous skin lesion showing numerous `Mycobacterium leprae` bacilli. Best direct institutional image source for an organism-centered story.

## Drafting notes

- Be explicit that `lepra` started as a descriptive skin word, not a microbiologic label.
- Spell out the grammatical point in plain English: `leprae` means `of leprosy`.
- Keep the old-name/new-microbe contrast central.
- Avoid implying ancient texts were precisely describing the same disease entity modern clinicians mean by Hansen's disease.
- Use the CDC and PMC material to keep the ending grounded in real transmission, treatment, and genomic history rather than pure stigma commentary.
