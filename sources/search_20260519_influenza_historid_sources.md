# Influenza etymology HistorID source notes

## Sources checked

1. Online Etymology Dictionary: Influenza
   URL: https://www.etymonline.com/word/influenza
   Key details: Traces from Italian influenza ("influence of the stars"), from Medieval Latin influentia ("a flowing in"), from Latin influere. First English use 1743 during European epidemic that began in Italy. Notes that the word was applied to diseases in Italian since at least 1504 on the notion of astral, occult, or atmospheric influence. The English word "influence" entered in the late 14th century as an astrological term meaning "streaming ethereal power from the stars acting upon human character or destiny."

2. The Gentleman's Magazine, April 1743
   URL: https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015027523691
   Key details: First recorded English use of the word "influenza." The magazine reported from Rome: "An Article from Rome informs us that a Sort of Plague has broke out there, which destroys Abundance of their People, and they call it the Influenza." This represents the word's entry into English during an epidemic that spread from Italy across Europe.

3. Treccani: Influenza
   URL: https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/influenza/
   Key details: Authoritative Italian etymological source. Traces influenza through early 16th-century medical usage, confirming the astrological origin: influenza di stelle (influence of the stars). Notes the word originally referred to any epidemic disease attributed to celestial influence before narrowing to the specific respiratory illness.

4. Potter CW. A history of influenza. J Appl Microbiol. 2001;91(4):572-579.
   URL: https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-2672.2001.01492.x
   Key details: Academic review of influenza history. Covers the etymology, early descriptions (Hippocrates 412 BCE as earliest probable record), the word's journey through European languages, and the various historical names for the disease (grippe, catarrh, ague). Provides context for how the Renaissance astrological word survived into modern virology.

5. Wellcome Collection: Astrological man with signs of the zodiac, 1529
   URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Astrological_man_with_signs_of_the_zodiac,_1529_Wellcome_L0006175.jpg
   Key details: Renaissance woodcut showing zodiac signs mapped to human body parts. Published 1529, right in the era when influenza entered Italian medical vocabulary (~1504). Illustrates the exact astrological medical belief system that gave influenza its name. CC BY 4.0 license via Wellcome Collection.
