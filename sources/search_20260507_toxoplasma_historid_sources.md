# HistorID Source Notes: Before Cats Got the Blame

Date: 2026-05-07

## Historical discovery and naming

Source: Ferguson DJP. `Toxoplasma gondii`: 1908-2008, homage to Nicolle, Manceaux and Splendore. *Mem Inst Oswaldo Cruz*. 2009;104(2):133-148.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19430635/

Key evidence: Supports the key hinge that `Toxoplasma gondii` was discovered independently by Nicolle and Manceaux in 1908 and by Splendore in 1908.

Source: Kim K, Weiss LM. Toxoplasma: the next 100 years. *Microbes Infect*. 2008;10(9):978-984.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18672085/

Key evidence used: states that the parasite was initially described in Tunis by Nicolle and Manceaux in the tissues of the gundi (`Ctenodactylus gundi`) and in Brazil by Splendore in rabbit tissues.

Source: Dubey JP. The history of `Toxoplasma gondii`, the first 100 years. *J Eukaryot Microbiol*. 2008;55(6):467-475.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1550-7408.2008.00345.x

Key evidence: the parasite was first discovered in 1908 and named a year later; medical importance became clearer in 1939 with congenital infection; cats completed the life cycle in 1970.

Source: Innes EA. A brief history and overview of `Toxoplasma gondii`. *Zoonoses Public Health*. 2010;57(1):1-7.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1863-2378.2009.01276.x

Key evidence: confirms discovery in North Africa and Brazil; summarizes the later recognition of congenital disease, ocular disease, and the importance of cats as definitive hosts.

## Etymology

Source: Wiktionary. `Toxoplasma`.

Link: https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Toxoplasma

Key evidence: traces `Toxoplasma` to Greek `toxon` (bow) plus `plasma` (something formed or molded), referring to the bow-shaped form.

Verified framing used: `gondii` should be explained as a reference to the gundi rodent, not to cats.

## Life cycle and modern relevance

Source: Dubey JP. History of the discovery of the life cycle of `Toxoplasma gondii`. *Int J Parasitol*. 2009;39(8):877-882.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpara.2009.01.005

Key evidence: the full life cycle was not discovered until 1970, when felids were found to be the definitive host and the oocyst stage clarified how the parasite became globally prevalent.

Source: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. About toxoplasmosis. Updated March 11, 2025.

Link: https://www.cdc.gov/toxoplasmosis/about/index.html

Key evidence used:

- most people with healthy immune systems do not have symptoms
- serious disease matters especially in pregnancy and immunocompromised hosts
- transmission is broader than cats alone and includes undercooked meat, contaminated produce, soil, and congenital spread
