# Escherichia HistorID source notes

## Sources checked

1. Shulman ST, Friedmann HC, Sims RH. `Theodor Escherich: the first pediatric infectious diseases physician?` `Clin Infect Dis`. 2007;45(8):1025-1029.
   URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17879920/
   Key details: strong compact historical source. It explicitly describes Escherich as a pediatric infectious diseases pioneer and states that his bacteriologic studies identified the common colon bacillus that is now known as `Escherichia coli`.

2. Escherich T. `Die Darmbakterien des Säuglings und ihre Beziehungen zur Physiologie der Verdauung`. 1886.
   URL: https://archive.org/details/b28116410
   Key details: direct primary-source anchor for the 1886 monograph and the infant-gut setting of the discovery.

3. LPSN, `Escherichia`
   URL: https://lpsn.dsmz.de/genus/escherichia
   Key details: strongest source for the genus-name story. States that `Escherichia` was named after Theodor Escherich and that the genus was proposed in 1919 by Castellani and Chalmers.

4. LPSN, `Escherichia coli`
   URL: https://lpsn.dsmz.de/species/escherichia-coli
   Key details: strongest source for the species-name meaning. States that `coli` is the Latin genitive of `colon`, meaning `of the colon`.

5. Encyclopaedia Britannica, `E. coli`
   URL: https://www.britannica.com/science/E-coli
   Key details: useful modern framing of the organism as a normal intestinal resident with major pathogenic potential.

6. Online Etymology Dictionary, `E. coli`
   URL: https://www.etymonline.com/word/E.%20coli
   Key details: confirms later abbreviation usage and the same two-part naming logic in plain language.

7. Wikimedia Commons, `Escherich, Theodor.jpg`
   URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Escherich,_Theodor.jpg
   Key details: public-domain portrait suitable for a naming-story HistorID hero image.

## Drafting notes

- Keep the opening focused on Escherich and the naming, not on baby stool as a joke.
- The best irony is that the first name, `Bacterium coli commune`, was almost aggressively plain compared with the organism's later fame.
- The core teaching point is that the same organism can be a normal intestinal resident and a major pathogen, which makes the naming story clinically relevant rather than merely taxonomic.
