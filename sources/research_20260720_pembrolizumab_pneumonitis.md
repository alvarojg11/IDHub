# Research Notes — Pembrolizumab Pneumonitis (CIP)

Case: `pembrolizumab-pneumonitis` — "Friendly Fire After the Infusion"
Date: 2026-07-20

## Topic
Checkpoint inhibitor pneumonitis (CIP) as a mimic of infection. Pembrolizumab (anti-PD-1) monotherapy in a head and neck SCC patient; Grade 3 (hypoxemic) CIP; diagnosis of exclusion; corticosteroid-based management.

## Key clinical facts used (consensus across ASCO/SITC/ESMO/NCCN)

### Epidemiology / onset
- CIP incidence ~3-5% with anti-PD-1 monotherapy; higher with combination (e.g., ipi+nivo).
- Median onset ~2-3 months after starting therapy (Naidoo), but range is wide; can occur after first dose, many months in, or weeks after discontinuation.
- Class effect: PD-1, PD-L1, and CTLA-4 inhibitors. Mechanism = immune activation (T-cell disinhibition), NOT immunosuppression.

### Radiographic patterns (Naidoo classification, JCO 2017)
Five overlapping patterns; none specific:
1. Organizing pneumonia (OP / COP-like)
2. Nonspecific interstitial pneumonia (NSIP)-like
3. Hypersensitivity pneumonitis (HP)-like
4. Acute interstitial pneumonia (AIP) / ARDS
5. Pneumonitis, not otherwise specified (NOS)
- Bilateral ground-glass opacity is the most common presentation but overlaps with infection, edema, drug-induced lung disease.
- NOTE: Sources disagree on which single pattern is most frequent (OP vs NSIP); the case phrases this cautiously and does not assert a single "most common" pattern to avoid error.

### Diagnosis
- Diagnosis of exclusion: compatible exposure + compatible clinicoradiologic syndrome + reasonable exclusion of infection.
- Bronchoscopy with BAL in hospitalized/hypoxemic patients: bacterial/mycobacterial/fungal cultures, respiratory viral panel, cytology, cell differential.
- BAL in CIP: lymphocytic predominance, no pathogen. Eosinophilic predominance -> reconsider drug-induced eosinophilic pneumonia or parasitic disease (e.g., Strongyloides).
- Procalcitonin: low/normal supportive hint against typical bacterial pneumonia, but NOT diagnostic; never sole basis for steroids.
- Lung biopsy: not required; reserved for atypical/unclear cases.

### CTCAE grading -> management (ASCO 2021 update / SITC / ESMO)
- G1 (asymptomatic, radiographic only): hold ICI; close surveillance; repeat imaging ~3-4 weeks; consider steroids if not improving.
- G2 (symptomatic, limits instrumental ADLs): hold ICI; oral prednisone ~1 mg/kg/day; hospitalize if needed.
- G3 (severe, limits self-care ADLs, hypoxemia requiring O2): hold ICI; hospitalize; IV methylprednisolone 1-2 mg/kg/day.
- G4 (life-threatening): ICU; IV methylprednisolone; generally permanent discontinuation of drug class.

### Refractory CIP (no improvement at 48-72 h)
- Add second-line: infliximab 5 mg/kg (1-2 doses) OR mycophenolate mofetil; less commonly cyclophosphamide, tocilizumab, IVIG.
- Infliximab/mycophenolate are NOT first-line.
- Slow steroid taper >=6 weeks (often longer) to avoid recurrence.

### Re-challenge
- May consider for G1-2 that resolves to baseline; generally AVOID after G3-4 pneumonitis.

### Differential (esp. head & neck cancer)
- Typical/atypical CAP, aspiration pneumonia (dysphagia, prior chemoradiation), radiation pneumonitis (within field, within ~6-12 months), PJP (if steroids/immunosuppression), PE, lymphangitic carcinomatosis, drug-induced eosinophilic pneumonia.
