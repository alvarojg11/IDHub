# Histoplasma capsulatum HistorID source notes

## Sources checked

1. Darling ST. A protozoon general infection producing pseudotubercules in the lungs and focal necrosis in the liver, spleen, and lymphnodes.
   URL: https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.1906.62510440037003
   Key details: Primary discovery paper from JAMA 1906. Darling describes finding a protozoan-like organism in an autopsy of a 27-year-old carpenter from Martinique who died of what appeared to be miliary tuberculosis. He named it Histoplasma capsulatum based on its intracellular location in histiocytes and the clear halo he interpreted as a capsule.

2. Mahajan M. Etymologia: Histoplasma capsulatum.
   URL: https://doi.org/10.3201/eid2703.et2703
   Key details: CDC Emerging Infectious Diseases etymology note confirming that Darling serendipitously discovered the organism in 1905 while studying malaria during the Panama Canal construction. Confirms the name breakdown (Histo = histiocyte-like, plasma = cytoplasm, capsulatum = having a capsule, a misnomer). Notes it is a dimorphic fungus, not a protozoan.

3. DeMonbreun WA. The cultivation and cultural characteristics of Darling's Histoplasma capsulatum.
   URL: https://doi.org/10.4269/ajtmh.1934.s1-14.93
   Key details: The 1934 paper where DeMonbreun successfully cultured H. capsulatum and proved it was a dimorphic fungus, growing as mold at room temperature and budding yeast at body temperature. This corrected Darling's initial protozoan misclassification.

4. Kauffman CA. Histoplasmosis: a clinical and laboratory update.
   URL: https://doi.org/10.1128/CMR.00027-06
   Key details: Comprehensive clinical review covering the spectrum of histoplasmosis from asymptomatic to disseminated disease. Confirms the Ohio and Mississippi River valleys as highly endemic areas where 80-90% of residents show exposure by skin testing. Notes histoplasmosis as an AIDS-defining illness.

5. Wikipedia: Histoplasma capsulatum
   URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histoplasma_capsulatum
   Key details: Confirms Darling discovered the organism in 1906, the etymology section explicitly states the capsulatum name is a misnomer (refractive halo mimicking a capsule). Provides taxonomic and ecological context including dimorphic nature and endemic zones.

6. Wikipedia: Samuel Taylor Darling
   URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Taylor_Darling
   Key details: Biographical information. Darling born 1872, died 1925 in Beirut in a car accident. Darling Foundation Prize for malaria research established in his memory. Confirms he was a pathologist and bacteriologist who discovered Histoplasma capsulatum in Panama in 1906.

7. Wikimedia Commons: Histopathology of histoplasma, HE stain
   URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Histopathology_of_histoplasma,_HE_stain.png
   Key details: Hero image showing Histoplasma capsulatum in tissue (H&E stain) with the characteristic clear halo around each organism that Darling interpreted as a capsule. Public domain.

## Drafting notes

- The strongest hook is the double misnomer: both halves of the name are wrong, and both errors survived.
- Darling was studying malaria when he made the discovery. The serendipity is part of the story.
- The Panama Canal construction context matters: it was a medical crisis, workers dying from infectious diseases, Ancon Hospital at the center.
- DeMonbreun's 1934 culture proof is the pivot: "Darling had been right about the morphology and wrong about the biology."
- The "capsule" is a shrinkage artifact from tissue fixation. Make this specific.
- The endemicity data (80-90% exposure in Kansas City) anchors the modern relevance.
- The name-as-fossil metaphor is the closing idea: names outlast the theories that produced them.
- Keep the tone conversational but precise. Avoid the word "ironic" - let the facts do the work.
- Match the tone of the rotavirus wheel story (the-wheel-in-the-microscope) and the malaria bad air story (when-malaria-meant-bad-air).
