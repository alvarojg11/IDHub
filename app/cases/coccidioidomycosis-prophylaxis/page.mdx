<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    This case highlights the distinction between primary antifungal prophylaxis and
    pre-emptive therapy for coccidioidomycosis in people with HIV living in endemic
    regions. Those are not the same decision.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    For patients who are serologically negative, routine primary antifungal prophylaxis
    is not recommended, even when the CD4 count is below 250 cells/mm^3. Instead,
    yearly or twice-yearly serologic screening can be considered for people with HIV
    who live in endemic areas or who previously lived in or traveled through those
    regions.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    A new positive IgM or IgG serologic result in a previously negative patient with
    HIV and CD4 count below 250 cells/mm^3 changes the situation. That result raises
    concern for possible active coccidioidomycosis and should trigger a focused clinical
    evaluation for pulmonary, meningeal, osseous, or other extrapulmonary disease.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    If no signs, symptoms, imaging findings, or laboratory abnormalities compatible
    with active coccidioidomycosis are identified, pre-emptive therapy with fluconazole
    400 mg PO once daily is recommended. This approach should be continued until the
    CD4 count is at least 250 cells/mm^3 and virologic suppression on ART is documented.
    By contrast, if the patient already has CD4 count at or above 250 cells/mm^3 with
    viral suppression, close follow-up without antifungal therapy is generally preferred.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    One additional pearl: serology is useful for screening and follow-up, but it cannot
    exclude disease in a symptomatic or highly suspicious presentation because antibodies
    may take time to develop, especially in immunocompromised hosts.
  </p>
</div>

    <li>
      In endemic areas, yearly or twice-yearly coccidioidal serology can be considered
      for people with HIV who previously tested negative.
    </li>
    <li>
      Primary antifungal prophylaxis is not recommended for serologically negative
      patients solely because they live in an endemic area and have a low CD4 count.
    </li>
    <li>
      A new positive coccidioidal IgM or IgG result in a previously negative patient
      with CD4 count below 250 cells/mm^3 should prompt evaluation for active disease.
    </li>
    <li>
      If that evaluation does not reveal active coccidioidomycosis, use fluconazole
      400 mg PO once daily as pre-emptive therapy.
    </li>
    <li>
      Stop pre-emptive therapy once the CD4 count is at least 250 cells/mm^3 and HIV
      is virologically suppressed on ART.
    </li>
    <li>
      Counsel patients in endemic regions to avoid major dust exposure when possible,
      use appropriate N95 protection around soil excavation, and stay indoors during
