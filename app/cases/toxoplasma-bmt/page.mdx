<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The diagnosis of toxoplasma encephalitis rests on the convergence of clinical, epidemiological, and radiological findings rather than a single confirmatory result. Multiple ring-enhancing lesions concentrated in the basal ganglia, a lymphocyte-predominant CSF pleocytosis, day 13 after myeloablative allo-HSCT with no TMP-SMX prophylaxis, and the systematic exclusion of competing diagnoses together make toxoplasma reactivation the leading and most actionable diagnosis. HHV-6 encephalitis was a reasonable concern given the timing, but HHV-6 produces limbic FLAIR signal change rather than discrete ring-enhancing masses with mass effect and midline shift. Undetectable EBV PCR substantially lowers the likelihood of EBV-driven PTLD. Negative galactomannan and beta-D-glucan argue against Aspergillus. No identifiable bacterial source is present. Confirmatory testing, including serum and CSF Toxoplasma PCR and serum IgG, should be sent, but empirical treatment must not wait: the clinical and radiological picture in an unprotected allo-HSCT recipient is sufficient to begin therapy immediately.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    <em>Toxoplasma gondii</em> is an obligate intracellular protozoan parasite. The definitive host is the felid; cats shed oocysts in feces after primary infection, and these oocysts can survive in soil for months to years. Humans acquire primary infection through ingestion of oocysts from the environment or through consumption of undercooked meat containing tissue cysts (bradyzoites). During acute primary infection, rapidly replicating tachyzoites disseminate through the bloodstream and eventually penetrate the brain, skeletal muscle, and myocardium, where they convert to slowly replicating bradyzoites forming tissue cysts. These cysts persist for the lifetime of the host. In immunocompetent individuals, CD8+ T cells continuously suppress cyst reactivation, and no disease results. When cellular immunity is profoundly depleted, as occurs after myeloablative conditioning and during the early post-transplant period, bradyzoites reconvert to tachyzoites, causing focal necrotizing encephalitis without any requirement for new environmental exposure.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The window between transplant and the initiation of TMP-SMX prophylaxis (approximately day 42, when engraftment is confirmed) represents the period of highest risk for seropositive recipients. TMP-SMX is withheld during this window to avoid myelosuppression at a time when engraftment is still underway. Pretransplant Toxoplasma IgG testing is a standard component of the infectious disease pretransplant evaluation and identifies seropositive recipients who require close neurological surveillance during this window. In this case, the missed pretransplant check meant that no heightened monitoring was in place, and early symptoms were attributed to tacrolimus toxicity rather than prompting urgent neuroimaging.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    First-line treatment for CNS toxoplasmosis is pyrimethamine (200 mg loading dose, then 50 to 75 mg daily) combined with sulfadiazine (1 to 1.5 g every 6 hours) and leucovorin (10 to 25 mg daily). Pyrimethamine and sulfadiazine act synergistically by blocking sequential steps in the parasite's folate synthesis pathway. Leucovorin is co-administered to prevent the bone marrow toxicity that pyrimethamine produces in humans by bypassing the enzyme it inhibits. Pyrimethamine combined with clindamycin is used when sulfa drugs are contraindicated. Corticosteroids for perilesional edema should be used sparingly, as they compound immunosuppression; they are reserved for cases with significant mass effect and impending herniation.
  </p>

<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Teaching Points
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <ul className="list-disc pl-6 text-lg leading-7 text-gray-800 space-y-2">
    <li>
      Toxoplasma IgG must be checked in every allo-HSCT candidate as part of the pretransplant infectious disease evaluation. A positive result identifies the recipient as seropositive and triggers the monitoring and prophylaxis protocol below.
    </li>
    <li>
      Seropositive recipients should undergo weekly blood Toxoplasma PCR monitoring from day 0 through day 42. A rising or positive PCR allows pre-emptive treatment before clinical disease develops, preventing the progression to encephalitis seen here.
    </li>
    <li>
      TMP-SMX is withheld until approximately day 42 when engraftment is confirmed, to avoid myelosuppression during the critical engraftment window. This creates a defined high-risk period that makes pretransplant serology and weekly PCR surveillance essential, not optional.
    </li>
    <li>
      Reactivation requires no new environmental exposure: latent bradyzoite cysts persist in the brain indefinitely and convert to invasive tachyzoites when T-cell immunity is ablated by conditioning and immunosuppression.
    </li>
    <li>
      First-line treatment is pyrimethamine plus sulfadiazine plus leucovorin. Leucovorin must not be omitted. Pyrimethamine plus clindamycin is the sulfa-allergy alternative. TMP-SMX is prophylaxis, not first-line acute therapy.
    </li>
  </ul>
