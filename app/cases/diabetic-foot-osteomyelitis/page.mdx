<CaseReveal pollId="case-diabetic-foot-osteomyelitis-q3">
<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Diabetic foot osteomyelitis (DFO) represents one of the most challenging
    complications of diabetic foot ulcers, occurring in up to 20% of moderate to
    severe infections. The diagnosis should be suspected when a probe-to-bone test
    is positive or when plain radiographs show cortical erosion, periosteal reaction,
    or bone destruction. MRI is the imaging modality of choice, with sensitivity
    exceeding 90% for detecting osteomyelitis and defining anatomic extent.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Empiric antibiotic therapy must cover Staphylococcus aureus (including MRSA
    in patients with risk factors), streptococci, Enterobacteriaceae, and
    anaerobes. The IDSA/IWGDF 2024 guidelines recommend against routine
    anti-pseudomonal coverage unless specific risk factors exist: recent
    hospitalization, prior Pseudomonas isolation, or severe infection in a
    warm climate with high Pseudomonas prevalence. A combination such as
    vancomycin plus cefepime provides appropriate breadth without unnecessary
    pseudomonal coverage.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Surgical debridement is indicated when there is extensive bone destruction,
    abscess formation, or necrotic tissue. Transmetatarsal amputation with
    resection of infected bone (even with margins showing osteomyelitis) permits shorter antibiotic duration (3 weeks)
    compared to medical therapy alone or incomplete debridement (often ≥4-6 weeks).
    The key principle is that adequate surgical source control reduces the
    bacterial burden and permits shorter antimicrobial courses.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    After surgical resection of infected bone (even with margins showing osteomyelitis), monitoring focuses on clinical evidence of response rather than routine imaging. Inflammatory markers (CRP, ESR) may provide supportive information but should not be used as sole stopping criteria. The decision to stop antibiotics at 3 weeks is based on clinical assessment showing no evidence of persistent infection, combined with dedicated wound care and optimization of diabetes and vascular disease. MRI is not routinely indicated when source control is complete.
  </p>
</div>

    </li>
    <li>
      Empiric antibiotic coverage should target Staphylococcus aureus (including MRSA

      anti-pseudomonal coverage is not recommended unless specific risk factors exist.
    </li>
    <li>
      Oral antibiotic therapy with good bioavailability (e.g., cephalexin, cefadroxil, doxycycline for MSSA,
      linezolid, TMP/SMX for MRSA) is appropriate after initial IV therapy and can complete
      the 3-week course.
    </li>
    <li>
      After surgical resection of infected bone (even with margins showing osteomyelitis), monitoring relies on clinical assessment rather than routine MRI. Clinical evidence of response at 3 weeks determines antibiotic discontinuation.
    </li>
    <li>
      Multidisciplinary care involving infectious diseases, vascular surgery,
      podiatry, and endocrinology is essential for optimal outcomes in diabetic
      foot osteomyelitis.
    </li>
