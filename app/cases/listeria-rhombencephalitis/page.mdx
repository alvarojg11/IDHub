<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Listeria rhombencephalitis is uncommon but high-risk and can occur in patients without
    classic chronic immunocompromising conditions. Pregnancy and the early postpartum
    period can alter cellular immunity and should not be dismissed as low risk when a
    patient presents with fever plus progressive cranial neuropathies or long-tract signs.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The syndrome is frequently biphasic: a nonspecific prodrome followed by brainstem
    neurologic deficits. Key clues in this case are fever, headache, bulbar dysfunction,
    ocular motor abnormalities, hemiparesis, and MRI evidence of medullary/pontine
    inflammatory lesions with abscess formation. Meningeal signs may be absent.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    CSF Gram stain and culture can be negative in neurolisteriosis, so molecular testing
    and MRI findings are critical. Once identified, therapy should include high-dose IV
    ampicillin with gentamicin synergy when feasible, with prolonged treatment in severe
    rhombencephalitis or abscess disease. Because bulbar involvement can rapidly cause
    aspiration and respiratory failure, airway monitoring and multidisciplinary supportive
    care are central components of management.
  </p>
</div>

<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Teaching points
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <ul className="list-disc pl-6 text-lg leading-7 text-gray-800 space-y-2">
    <li>
      Listeria rhombencephalitis can affect immunocompetent adults and may present as a
      stroke mimic with cranial neuropathies and hemiparesis.
    </li>
    <li>
      Absence of neck stiffness does not exclude severe CNS listeriosis.
    </li>
    <li>
      Negative CSF Gram stain or culture does not rule out Listeria; PCR and MRI can be
      decisive.
    </li>
    <li>
      Cephalosporins do not reliably cover Listeria; targeted treatment is ampicillin-based,
      often with gentamicin synergy.
    </li>
    <li>
      Bulbar dysfunction in brainstem disease raises immediate risk of aspiration and
      respiratory failure and should trigger close airway surveillance.
    </li>
  </ul>
</div>
