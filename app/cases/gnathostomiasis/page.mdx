<CaseReveal pollId="case-gnathostomiasis-q1">
<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Gnathostomiasis is a food-borne parasitic zoonosis caused by nematodes of the genus
    <em> Gnathostoma</em>. Humans acquire infection after ingesting larvae in raw or undercooked
    intermediate or paratenic hosts, most commonly fish, shrimp, crab, frog, or chicken.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    In humans, larvae do not mature into adult worms. Instead, they penetrate the gastric wall and
    migrate unpredictably through tissues. The most common manifestation is intermittent migratory
    edema of the skin and subcutaneous tissues, often accompanied by erythema, pain, and a
    piercing sensation. Peripheral eosinophilia is common but not universal.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The epidemiologic clue is critical. In this case, recurrent migratory edema following
    consumption of raw seafood in an endemic region is much more suggestive of gnathostomiasis than
    simple angioedema or bacterial skin infection. Ocular and central nervous system involvement can
    occur and are the most feared complications.
  </p>
</div>
