<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The clinical pattern is Pneumocystis pneumonia: slowly progressive dyspnea, dry cough, exertional hypoxemia out of proportion to a quiet lung exam, elevated LDH, and diffuse bilateral ground-glass opacities. The hidden clue is impaired cellular immunity. In this case, HIV antigen/antibody testing later returns positive and the CD4 count is 38 cells/uL.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    <em>Pneumocystis jirovecii</em> is an opportunistic fungus adapted to humans. It was historically classified as a protozoan, which is why older terminology and some older teaching can be confusing. Human disease is caused by <em>P. jirovecii</em>; <em>Pneumocystis carinii</em> refers to a related organism in rats and should not be used as the human pathogen name.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    In the lung, Pneumocystis organisms occupy the alveolar space as trophic forms and cyst or ascus forms. The host inflammatory response and organisms produce a foamy, proteinaceous intra-alveolar exudate that interferes with gas exchange. This explains a classic bedside mismatch: the patient can be profoundly hypoxemic while auscultation remains nearly normal and the chest radiograph is subtle early. CT is more sensitive and commonly shows bilateral ground-glass opacities.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Disease is strongly linked to defects in T-cell immunity. Advanced HIV with CD4 count below 200 cells/uL is the classic risk state, but Pneumocystis pneumonia also occurs after solid organ or hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, hematologic malignancy, prolonged high-dose corticosteroids, and other T-cell-suppressive therapies. Pneumocystis may be acquired through airborne transmission or carried transiently, but clinically important disease usually reflects failure of cellular immune control rather than ordinary community exposure alone.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Pneumocystis is not diagnosed by routine bacterial culture. Diagnosis depends on detecting the organism or its nucleic acid in a respiratory specimen. Induced sputum can be used when available, but bronchoalveolar lavage has higher sensitivity, especially when the diagnosis is uncertain or the patient is significantly hypoxemic. Direct fluorescent antibody testing, silver stains such as Gomori methenamine silver, and PCR are commonly used. Serum beta-D-glucan is frequently elevated because Pneumocystis has fungal cell wall components, but the test is nonspecific and can also be positive with other invasive fungal infections or false-positive exposures.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole is first-line treatment. For HIV-associated Pneumocystis pneumonia, the usual treatment duration is 21 days. Adjunctive corticosteroids reduce respiratory deterioration and mortality in moderate-to-severe disease and should be started when room-air PaO2 is less than 70 mmHg or the alveolar-arterial oxygen gradient is at least 35 mmHg. After HIV is confirmed, antiretroviral therapy is generally started during the acute PJP treatment course, often within 2 weeks once the patient is clinically stabilized.
  </p>
</div>

<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Pathogen Notes
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <ul className="list-disc pl-6 text-lg leading-7 text-gray-800 space-y-2">
    <li>
      <em>Pneumocystis jirovecii</em> is a fungus and an opportunistic pulmonary pathogen of humans.
    </li>
    <li>
      The organism is host-adapted: <em>P. jirovecii</em> causes human disease, while <em>P. carinii</em> is the rat species name.
    </li>
    <li>
      Pneumocystis forms live in alveoli and produce a foamy intra-alveolar exudate that impairs oxygen diffusion.
    </li>
    <li>
      The classic radiographic pattern is bilateral ground-glass opacity, often with hypoxemia more impressive than the lung exam.
    </li>
    <li>
      Major risk states include CD4 T-cell depletion from advanced HIV, transplant immunosuppression, hematologic malignancy, prolonged corticosteroids, and T-cell-suppressive biologics.
    </li>
    <li>
      Diagnosis requires Pneumocystis-directed testing on induced sputum or BAL fluid; routine bacterial cultures do not identify the organism.
    </li>
    <li>
      Serum beta-D-glucan is sensitive but nonspecific and should be interpreted as supportive evidence rather than a stand-alone diagnosis.
    </li>
    <li>
      TMP-SMX is used for both treatment and prophylaxis. In HIV, prophylaxis is indicated when CD4 count is below 200 cells/uL or there is a prior episode of PJP.
    </li>
  </ul>
