<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The central diagnostic problem in this case is distinguishing recurrent cryptococcal disease from a new or evolving white matter process in a patient with advanced HIV who has recently reconstituted immunity on ART. The MRI pattern is the key clue. PML classically produces asymmetric subcortical white matter lesions that involve the U-fibers and are hyperintense on T2 and FLAIR sequences. In contrast, cryptococcal relapse more often produces meningeal disease, hydrocephalus, or gelatinous pseudocysts rather than a destructive frontal white matter lesion. Toxoplasmosis and primary CNS lymphoma remain important alternative diagnoses in advanced HIV, but their radiographic patterns are usually more nodular and mass-like.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    What makes this case specifically inflammatory PML is the timing after ART and the presence of enhancement and mass effect. Classic PML often has minimal enhancement and little edema. Once immune recovery begins, however, inflammatory infiltration can make lesions enlarge rapidly and even produce midline shift or herniation. A low-level positive JC virus PCR supports the diagnosis, but clinicians should remember that CSF PCR sensitivity is incomplete, particularly in early disease and PML-IRIS. In patients with convincing clinical and imaging features, a negative test should prompt repeat CSF analysis with a sensitive assay or consideration of brain biopsy.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Management centers on maintaining ART because immune reconstitution is the only intervention consistently associated with improved survival in HIV-related PML. The literature on corticosteroids in PML-IRIS is limited to case reports, case series, and retrospective cohorts, with mixed results and no proven survival benefit overall. Even so, most reviews support using steroids selectively when the inflammatory response is causing severe edema, enhancement, declining neurologic status, or mass effect. This patient has subfalcine herniation, so the immediate priority is to control the inflammatory swelling while continuing ART and standard supportive neurologic care such as seizure management. The prior history of cryptococcal meningitis is an important distractor, but the normal opening pressure, negative fungal studies, and incompatible MRI pattern argue against relapse as the driver of his current deterioration.
  </p>
</div>

  Teaching Points

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <ul className="list-disc pl-6 text-lg leading-7 text-gray-800 space-y-2">
    <li>
      <strong>PML-IRIS should be suspected when focal neurologic deficits worsen after ART initiation in a patient whose HIV viremia is improving.</strong> Enhancement, edema, and mass effect are atypical for classic PML and should raise concern for an inflammatory form rather than pure viral progression.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>The MRI pattern helps separate PML from other advanced HIV brain lesions.</strong> Asymmetric subcortical white matter disease involving U-fibers favors PML, whereas recurrent cryptococcosis is more often meningeal and toxoplasmosis or lymphoma usually produce more nodular enhancing masses.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>CSF JC virus PCR is helpful but not perfectly sensitive.</strong> A negative result does not exclude PML-IRIS when imaging and clinical course are strongly suggestive; repeat testing with a more sensitive assay or brain biopsy may be necessary.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>ART should usually be continued in HIV-associated PML.</strong> Corticosteroids are reserved for severe inflammatory PML with edema, enhancement, mass effect, or impending herniation, not as routine therapy for every case, because available steroid data are observational and have not shown clear overall outcome benefit.
    </li>
  </ul>
