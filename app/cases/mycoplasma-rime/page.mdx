<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The key clinical clue is severe erosive mucositis out of proportion to the degree of cutaneous involvement after a febrile respiratory prodrome. Recent trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole use appropriately raises concern for Stevens-Johnson syndrome, but classic drug-induced Stevens-Johnson syndrome or toxic epidermal necrolysis usually features more extensive epidermal injury and broader skin involvement. In this patient, the faint pulmonary findings, limited body surface area involvement, and mucosa-predominant presentation favor reactive infectious mucocutaneous eruption.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Respiratory multiplex polymerase chain reaction later detected Mycoplasma pneumoniae, supporting Mycoplasma-associated RIME. Management centers on aggressive supportive care, including intravenous fluids, pain control, topical oral care, and nutritional support, along with treatment of the associated atypical respiratory infection. Because this patient was hospitalized and unable to tolerate oral medications, intravenous azithromycin was the most appropriate antimicrobial choice. Oral doxycycline is also active against Mycoplasma pneumoniae, but route matters in a patient with severe odynophagia.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The term RIME broadened the older Mycoplasma-induced rash and mucositis concept to include similar mucositis-predominant eruptions triggered by several infections. For board purposes, you should recognize Mycoplasma pneumoniae as the classic trigger and distinguish RIME from Stevens-Johnson syndrome or toxic epidermal necrolysis by its respiratory prodrome, sparse cutaneous disease, and prominent mucositis. Other extrapulmonary manifestations of Mycoplasma pneumoniae include hemolytic anemia, neurologic syndromes such as encephalitis, and cardiac complications such as myocarditis.
  </p>

<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Teaching Points
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <ul className="list-disc pl-6 text-lg leading-7 text-gray-800 space-y-2">
    <li>
      <strong>Reactive infectious mucocutaneous eruption is a mucositis-predominant syndrome with relatively limited skin disease.</strong> Patients typically present with painful oral, ocular, or urogenital mucosal involvement, while cutaneous lesions may be sparse or even absent.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Mycoplasma pneumoniae remains the classic trigger and should be actively sought when RIME follows a respiratory prodrome.</strong> Confirmatory testing often follows the bedside diagnosis, so recognition of the syndrome should not wait for microbiology to return.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>The modern term RIME is broader than the older Mycoplasma-induced rash and mucositis label.</strong> It captures mucositis-predominant parainfectious eruptions triggered by several pathogens, although Mycoplasma pneumoniae remains the best known board-relevant association.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Management combines aggressive supportive care with treatment of the associated atypical pneumonia when Mycoplasma pneumoniae is suspected.</strong> Intravenous azithromycin is a practical inpatient option when oral intake is poor, while hydration, analgesia, and careful airway monitoring remain essential.
    </li>
  </ul>
