<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Daptomycin-associated eosinophilic pneumonia is an uncommon but clinically important drug-induced lung disease. Most reported cases occur in patients receiving prolonged therapy for deep gram-positive infections such as osteomyelitis, bacteremia, endocarditis, or prosthetic material infections. The syndrome typically emerges after 2 to 4 weeks of exposure, although earlier and later presentations have been described. Older age, higher cumulative exposure, and reduced renal clearance have been proposed as risk factors, but the diagnosis remains primarily clinical because no single laboratory test establishes causality.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The proposed mechanism is an immune-mediated pulmonary reaction after daptomycin accumulates in the alveolar space and interacts with pulmonary surfactant. This is distinct from the pharmacologic reason daptomycin is not used for primary pneumonia: surfactant inhibits its antibacterial activity. In eosinophilic pneumonia, the clinical phenotype is inflammatory rather than microbiologic failure, with fever, dyspnea, hypoxemia, diffuse or peripheral opacities on chest imaging, and eosinophilic inflammation in the airspaces. CT findings are variable and can include ground-glass opacities, consolidations, reticular changes, and occasionally pleural effusions, so imaging alone is supportive but not diagnostic.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Diagnostic criteria commonly used in the literature include current daptomycin exposure, fever, dyspnea with increased oxygen requirement, new pulmonary infiltrates, bronchoalveolar lavage eosinophilia greater than 25%, exclusion of alternative causes, and clinical improvement after daptomycin withdrawal. Peripheral eosinophilia is helpful when present but may be delayed or absent early. The differential diagnosis includes breakthrough bacterial pneumonia, septic pulmonary emboli, Pneumocystis pneumonia, organizing pneumonia, acute eosinophilic pneumonia from other exposures, and parasitic pulmonary syndromes such as Strongyloides hyperinfection. BAL eosinophilia and the temporal relationship to daptomycin are the central discriminators.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Treatment centers on stopping daptomycin and avoiding rechallenge. Systemic corticosteroids are frequently used for patients with hypoxemia, extensive radiographic disease, or slow improvement after withdrawal, but the evidence base is derived largely from case reports and observational summaries rather than randomized trials. Clinical improvement often begins within days after discontinuation, with radiographic resolution occurring more gradually. Because daptomycin is usually being used for a serious gram-positive infection, management must also include selection of an alternative active regimen based on the original syndrome, susceptibility profile, renal function, and need for outpatient feasibility.
  </p>
</div>
