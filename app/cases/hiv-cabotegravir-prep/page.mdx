<CaseReveal pollId="case-hiv-cabotegravir-prep-q2">
<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Long-acting injectable cabotegravir (CAB-LA) is a highly effective PrEP option, but
    breakthrough HIV acquisition — though rare — does occur. The key clinical challenge
    in these cases is that cabotegravir is an integrase strand transfer inhibitor (InSTI),
    and HIV acquired in the presence of an InSTI may harbor InSTI resistance mutations.
    Cross-resistance within the InSTI class means that mutations selected by cabotegravir
    can compromise the activity of dolutegravir and bictegravir, the two InSTIs recommended
    for first-line ART.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The 2024 IAS-USA guidelines address this scenario directly: persons who acquired HIV
    after exposure to cabotegravir for PrEP should have a blood sample for InSTI genotyping
    drawn prior to beginning therapy with an InSTI-based regimen. If therapy is desired
    before genotype results are available — or if InSTI resistance is present or suspected —
    ritonavir- or cobicistat-boosted darunavir plus TXF/XTC should be used (evidence rating:
    AIIb). Boosted darunavir has a high barrier to resistance and belongs to a completely
    different drug class (protease inhibitor), making it unaffected by any potential InSTI
    mutations.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    This approach contrasts with breakthrough HIV in patients on oral TDF/FTC or TAF/FTC
    PrEP, where the concern is for NRTI resistance (M184V/I, K65R) rather than InSTI
    resistance. In those cases, the guidelines permit starting dolutegravir or bictegravir
    plus TXF/XTC before resistance testing returns, since the anchor drug (the InSTI) is
    not compromised by prior PrEP exposure.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Once the InSTI genotype confirms no resistance, the regimen should be transitioned to a
    recommended InSTI-based regimen — either BIC/TAF/FTC or dolutegravir plus TXF/XTC —
    which are preferred for long-term therapy due to superior tolerability, fewer drug-drug
    interactions, and lower metabolic burden compared with boosted protease inhibitor
    regimens.
  </p>
</div>

  Teaching points

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <ul className="list-disc pl-6 text-lg leading-7 text-gray-800 space-y-2">
    <li>
      When HIV is acquired during or after long-acting cabotegravir PrEP, InSTI resistance
      must be suspected until proven otherwise — draw an integrase genotype before starting
      an InSTI-based regimen.
    </li>
    <li>
      If ART is needed before InSTI genotype results return, use boosted darunavir (DRV/r
      or DRV/COBI) plus TXF/XTC as the initial regimen (IAS-USA 2024, evidence rating: AIIb).
    </li>
    <li>
      Do not start BIC/TAF/FTC, DTG-based, or any InSTI-based regimen empirically in this
      setting — cross-resistance among InSTIs means cabotegravir-selected mutations can
      compromise dolutegravir and bictegravir.
    </li>
    <li>
      This differs from breakthrough HIV on oral TDF/FTC or TAF/FTC PrEP, where InSTIs
      (dolutegravir or bictegravir) can be started before genotype results return because
      the InSTI class is not compromised by prior NRTI exposure.
    </li>
    <li>
      Once InSTI genotype excludes resistance, transition to a recommended first-line
      InSTI-based regimen (BIC/TAF/FTC or DTG + TXF/XTC) for long-term therapy.
    </li>
    <li>
      Rapid ART initiation remains the standard — do not delay therapy while waiting for
      genotype results. Start an appropriate non-InSTI regimen and modify once results
      are available.
    </li>
  </ul>
