<CaseReveal pollId="case-dengue-q2">
<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Dengue is the most important arboviral infection worldwide and is caused by any one
    of four dengue virus serotypes. Classic dengue often presents with abrupt fever,
    severe myalgias, retro-orbital pain, nausea, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, and mild
    transaminase elevation. In endemic settings, this syndrome should be high on the
    differential for an acute febrile illness during the rainy season.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The critical phase occurs during the 24 to 48 hours around defervescence, often
    around illness days 5 to 7. This is when patients at risk for severe dengue can
    suddenly deteriorate because of transient increased vascular permeability. Warning
    signs include abdominal pain or tenderness, persistent vomiting, lethargy or
    restlessness, mucosal bleeding, fluid accumulation, liver enlargement, and a rising
    hematocrit with a rapid platelet fall. This patient has several of these features,
    including hemoconcentration and narrowing pulse pressure, both of which suggest
    plasma leakage.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Management is supportive. Patients with warning signs should be observed closely,
    ideally in a monitored setting, with frequent reassessment of pulse pressure, urine
    output, hematocrit, and overall intravascular volume status. The main intervention is
    careful administration of isotonic crystalloid fluids. Too little fluid risks shock,
    while excessive fluid can cause harm once vascular leak begins to resolve.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    No specific antiviral therapy is available. Aspirin and other NSAIDs should be
    avoided because they can worsen bleeding risk. Prophylactic platelet transfusions
    are generally not useful and should be reserved for clinically significant bleeding
    rather than thrombocytopenia alone.
  </p>
</div>

<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Teaching points
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <ul className="list-disc pl-6 text-lg leading-7 text-gray-800 space-y-2">
    <li>
      Dengue ranges from a mild febrile illness to severe dengue with plasma leakage,
      shock, severe bleeding, or major organ involvement.
    </li>
    <li>
      The most dangerous period is often not the height of fever but the 24 to 48 hours
      around defervescence.
    </li>
    <li>
      A rising hematocrit with a falling platelet count is a high-yield clue to evolving
      plasma leakage.
    </li>
    <li>
      Warning signs such as abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, lethargy, mucosal
      bleeding, and narrow pulse pressure should trigger close monitoring and supportive
      care.
    </li>
    <li>
      Judicious isotonic crystalloid therapy is the cornerstone of management for
      patients entering the critical phase.
    </li>
    <li>
      Avoid aspirin and NSAIDs, and do not give prophylactic platelet transfusions for
      thrombocytopenia alone.
    </li>
  </ul>
