<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">Rationale</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Alemtuzumab is a potent CD52-directed lymphocyte-depleting antibody, and CMV is one of
    its most important opportunistic infectious complications. In the 2024 retrospective
    cohort by George, Mozessohn, and Lam, non-transplant patients with hematologic
    malignancies treated with alemtuzumab had a composite CMV infection rate of 42%, with
    symptomatic infection in 21%.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Timing matters. In that cohort, the median time to CMV infection was 39.5 days from
    the first dose, closely matching this patient&apos;s week-5 presentation. In a patient on
    alemtuzumab with fever and no clear bacterial source, CMV deserves early consideration
    even before PCR confirmation is available.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The same study showed major variation in screening and prevention practices, but most
    infections were identified by serum PCR during preemptive monitoring rather than after
    fulminant tissue-invasive disease. That makes early recognition essential in patients
    receiving alemtuzumab outside the transplant setting.
  </p>
</div>

<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">Teaching Points</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <ul className="list-disc pl-6 text-lg leading-7 text-gray-800 space-y-2">
    <li>
      Alemtuzumab used for lymphoproliferative disorders outside transplantation carries
      substantial CMV risk, not just in chronic lymphocytic leukemia.
    </li>
    <li>
      In the 2024 Sunnybrook cohort, CMV infection occurred in 42% of patients and was
      detected a median of 39.5 days after alemtuzumab initiation.
    </li>
    <li>
      Weekly serum CMV PCR during therapy is a reasonable preemptive strategy when using
      alemtuzumab, especially in CMV-seropositive patients.
    </li>
    <li>
      Stable patients with CMV DNAemia are often treated with oral valganciclovir, while
      severe or tissue-invasive disease may require intravenous ganciclovir.
    </li>
    <li>
      Standardized prevention protocols remain poorly defined in the non-transplant setting,
      so baseline CMV serology and consistent surveillance can reduce missed infections.
    </li>
  </ul>
</div>
