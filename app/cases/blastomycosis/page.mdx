<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Blastomycosis is caused by <em>Blastomyces</em> species, a dimorphic fungus endemic in
    parts of the Ohio and Mississippi River valleys, the Great Lakes region, and the
    southeastern United States. Infection usually begins after inhalation of conidia,
    with pulmonary disease ranging from asymptomatic infection to severe pneumonia.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Extrapulmonary disease follows hematogenous spread, and the skin is the most common
    site of dissemination. Verrucous or ulcerative cutaneous lesions together with
    pulmonary findings should prompt consideration of blastomycosis, especially in a
    patient from an endemic region. A key diagnostic clue is broad-based budding yeast on
    tissue examination.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    For mild to moderate pulmonary or disseminated disease without CNS involvement,
    itraconazole is the preferred treatment. Severe disease, CNS involvement, or clinical
    instability generally warrants initial liposomal amphotericin B followed by step-down
    oral azole therapy once the patient improves.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    In this case, the normal CSF evaluation and stable clinical status support treatment
    with oral itraconazole rather than upfront amphotericin B. Close clinical follow-up
    and interval imaging are appropriate to document response.
  </p>
</div>
