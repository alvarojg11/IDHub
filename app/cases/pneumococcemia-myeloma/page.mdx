<CaseReveal pollId="case-pneumococcemia-myeloma-q2">
<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Invasive pneumococcal disease in an adult should prompt consideration of an
    underlying host-defense problem, especially when the infection is accompanied by
    unexplained anemia or abnormal serum protein findings. Multiple myeloma is a
    particularly important consideration because clonal plasma-cell proliferation can
    suppress functional polyclonal immunoglobulin production, leaving patients
    vulnerable to encapsulated organisms such as <em>S. pneumoniae</em>.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The peripheral smear clue in this case is rouleaux formation. When rouleaux appears
    alongside anemia and hyperproteinemia, a monoclonal gammopathy should move high on
    the differential.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Confirmation requires studies that demonstrate and characterize the monoclonal
    protein burden and establish clonal marrow involvement. Serum protein electrophoresis,
    urine protein studies with immunofixation, serum free light chains, and bone marrow
    biopsy are therefore the appropriate next steps once the clinical pattern is
    recognized. Acute infection still requires standard antimicrobial management, but
    finding the underlying plasma-cell disorder changes both prognosis and long-term
    infection-prevention planning.
  </p>
</div>
