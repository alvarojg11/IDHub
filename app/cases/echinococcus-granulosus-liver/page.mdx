<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    <em>Echinococcus granulosus</em> sensu stricto is a small tapeworm maintained in a dog–sheep–dog life cycle. The definitive host — most commonly the domestic dog — harbors the adult tapeworm in the small intestine and sheds eggs in feces. Sheep and other livestock ingest these eggs on pasture; the eggs release oncospheres that penetrate the intestinal wall, enter the portal circulation, and lodge predominantly in the liver, where they develop into hydatid cysts over months to years. When a dog ingests infected offal containing cysts with protoscolices, the cycle completes as protoscolices develop into adult tapeworms. Humans are accidental intermediate hosts who acquire infection by ingesting eggs from contaminated food, water, soil, or direct hand-to-mouth contact with infected dogs. Person-to-person transmission does not occur.
  </p>
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The hydatid cyst has three layers: the outer pericyst, which is a host-derived fibrous capsule; the middle laminated membrane, which is an acellular chitin-like layer produced by the parasite that allows nutrient passage while protecting the cyst from host immune attack; and the inner germinal membrane, which gives rise to brood capsules containing protoscolices. Daughter cysts develop when brood capsules detach and form independent cystic structures within the mother cyst, creating the characteristic multivesicular or rosette appearance seen on imaging.
  </p>
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The WHO Informal Working Group on Echinococcosis (WHO-IWGE) classification system is the standard framework for staging hepatic cystic echinococcosis and guiding management decisions. Cysts are classified as CE1 through CE5 based on ultrasound morphology. CE1 cysts are active, unilocular, anechoic lesions with a double-wall sign. CE2 cysts are active, multivesicular lesions containing daughter cysts in a rosette or spoke-wheel pattern — which is what this patient has. CE3a cysts are transitional, with a detached laminated membrane producing the water lily sign. CE3b cysts are transitional, with a solid matrix and daughter cysts. CE4 cysts are inactive, with heterogeneous contents and no daughter cysts. CE5 cysts are inactive, with a thick calcified wall. Active and transitional cysts require treatment; inactive cysts (CE4 and CE5) are managed with watchful waiting and serial ultrasound surveillance.
  </p>
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The management of cystic echinococcosis is stage-specific, and this principle is the most important clinical takeaway. For CE1 cysts smaller than 5 cm, albendazole monotherapy for 6 months may be sufficient. For CE1 cysts 5 cm or larger and CE3a cysts, the PAIR procedure (puncture, aspiration, injection of scolecidal agent, re-aspiration) combined with albendazole cover is the preferred approach. PAIR uses a controlled, staged protocol: the cyst is punctured under imaging guidance, fluid is aspirated, a scolecidal agent such as 20% hypertonic saline or 95% ethanol is injected to kill protoscolices in situ, and the agent is then re-aspirated. Albendazole is started 4 days before the procedure and continued for 1 to 3 months afterward.
  </p>
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    CE2 and CE3b cysts, however, are not amenable to PAIR because the multivesicular structure prevents complete evacuation of all daughter cyst compartments. Residual daughter cysts may contain viable protoscolices that lead to recurrence. For these stages, surgical management is preferred, allowing complete removal of the cyst contents with direct visualization. Albendazole coverage before and after surgery follows the same timeline as for PAIR.
  </p>
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The cardinal contraindication to PAIR beyond cyst type is biliary communication. If the cyst communicates with the biliary tree, injecting a scolecidal agent such as hypertonic saline or ethanol into the cyst will cause sclerosing cholangitis — a devastating complication. For this reason, the aspirated fluid should be checked for bilirubin before any scolecidal agent is injected. A bilirubin concentration in the aspirate approaching the serum level confirms a cysto-biliary fistula and precludes the injection step.
  </p>
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Albendazole is the cornerstone of medical therapy. The dose is 10 to 15 mg/kg/day divided into two doses, up to a maximum of 800 mg/day. Absorption is dramatically enhanced by taking the drug with a fatty meal, increasing bioavailability approximately 4 to 5 fold, because albendazole is poorly soluble and requires bile-mediated dissolution. Monitoring includes liver function tests every 2 weeks for the first 2 months and then monthly, along with complete blood counts to detect hepatotoxicity and the rare but serious complication of neutropenia.
  </p>
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Praziquantel has a limited role in cystic echinococcosis. A single dose of 40 mg/kg is sometimes given preoperatively to damage protoscolices and reduce the risk of secondary echinococcosis in case of intraoperative spillage, but it is not a primary treatment. Its primary veterinary and public health role is in deworming definitive hosts — dogs — to interrupt the transmission cycle at the population level.
  </p>
</div>
