<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    This case illustrates an indolent prosthetic joint infection (PJI) following shoulder arthroplasty. The subtle presentation with persistent pain, minimal systemic symptoms, and only mild elevation of inflammatory markers is classic for Cutibacterium acnes, a slow‑growing gram‑positive rod that colonizes sebaceous glands. Shoulder PJI is disproportionately caused by this organism, especially in male patients with a history of acne.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Diagnosis requires a high index of suspicion and appropriate microbiologic techniques. Synovial fluid culture should be incubated for at least 14 days to allow growth of this fastidious organism. Histopathology from intraoperative tissue samples showing acute inflammation supports the diagnosis. Management hinges on surgical intervention: either one‑stage or two‑stage exchange arthroplasty, combined with prolonged antibiotic therapy tailored to susceptibility.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Cutibacterium acnes is often susceptible to penicillin, ceftriaxone, clindamycin, and doxycycline. Biofilm formation on prosthetic material necessitates implant removal for cure. The IDSA guidelines recommend surgical debridement with implant retention only for acute PJI (&lt;30 days symptoms); chronic infections like this one require exchange arthroplasty.
  </p>
</div>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <ul className="list-disc pl-6 text-lg leading-7 text-gray-800 space-y-2">
    <li>
      <strong>Cutibacterium acnes is a leading cause of indolent prosthetic joint infection after shoulder arthroplasty.</strong> This gram‑positive rod, part of the skin flora, enters deep tissues during surgery and can cause low‑grade infection with pain, stiffness, and minimal systemic signs months to years later.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Diagnosis requires prolonged culture incubation and a high index of suspicion.</strong> Synovial fluid and tissue cultures should be held for 10–14 days. Inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP) may be only mildly elevated. Histopathology showing acute inflammation supports infection even with negative cultures.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Surgical management is essential for cure.</strong> Chronic PJI requires implant removal with one‑stage or two‑stage exchange arthroplasty. Antibiotic therapy alone has high failure rates due to biofilm formation. The choice of exchange depends on bone stock, soft‑tissue quality, and surgeon expertise.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Antibiotic therapy should be guided by susceptibility testing.</strong> Cutibacterium acnes is typically susceptible to penicillin, ceftriaxone, clindamycin, and doxycycline. Treatment duration is usually 4–6 weeks after exchange arthroplasty, with some experts recommending additional oral suppression.
    </li>
  </ul>
