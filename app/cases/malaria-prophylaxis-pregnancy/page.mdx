<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Malaria is among the most dangerous infections a pregnant traveler can
    acquire. Pregnancy reduces cell-mediated immunity and increases parasite
    sequestration in the placenta, so both <em>P. falciparum</em> and the
    relapsing species <em>P. vivax</em> and <em>P. ovale</em> carry higher risks
    of severe disease, anemia, miscarriage, stillbirth, preterm delivery, and
    low birth weight. Prevention &mdash; through chemoprophylaxis and personal
    protection against the <em>Anopheles</em> vector &mdash; is therefore the
    central task, and the pregnant traveler's toolkit is narrow because most
    prophylactic drugs are either contraindicated or lack safety data in
    pregnancy.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Doxycycline is excluded throughout pregnancy because tetracyclines discolor
    fetal teeth and inhibit bone growth. Atovaquone-proguanil is not recommended
    because robust safety data in pregnancy are lacking. The 8-aminoquinolines
    primaquine and tafenoquine are excluded because fetal G6PD status cannot be
    determined in utero and a deficient fetus could hemolyze. Chloroquine is
    safe in pregnancy but is defeated by chloroquine-resistant <em>P.
    falciparum</em>, which is now endemic across sub-Saharan Africa, the Indian
    subcontinent, and most of Southeast Asia. That leaves <strong>mefloquine as
    the only chemoprophylactic agent recommended by CDC and WHO for
    chloroquine-resistant areas in pregnancy</strong>, endorsed for all
    trimesters on the strength of large studies showing no increase in
    congenital anomalies, stillbirth, or fetal death. The FDA removed the
    first-trimester prophylactic-use contraindication in 2022. Mefloquine is
    started two weeks before travel to achieve protective levels and to surface
    intolerable neuropsychiatric effects (anxiety, insomnia, vivid dreams, and
    rarely psychosis) before departure, so candidates should be screened for
    psychiatric and cardiac conduction disease.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The single exception that defeats even mefloquine is multi-drug resistance.
    Along the Thai&ndash;Cambodian (Greater Mekong) border, <em>P.
    falciparum</em> has acquired documented resistance to chloroquine,
    mefloquine, and in places artemisinin derivatives. Because mefloquine is the
    only effective, pregnancy-safe agent and it no longer works there, no
    chemoprophylactic option remains for that region. The correct advice for a
    pregnant traveler is to avoid the border leg entirely &mdash; defer the
    visit until after delivery or re-route to a region without documented
    resistance where mefloquine can still protect her. Stand-by self-treatment
    with an artemisinin combination is never a substitute for continuous
    prophylaxis in pregnancy, because malaria in pregnancy can deteriorate
    rapidly and demands urgent professional care.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The relapsing species add a final wrinkle that does not end at the airport.
    <em>P. vivax</em> and <em>P. ovale</em> deposit dormant hypnozoites in the
    liver that can reactivate weeks to months later, causing relapses long after
    return. Blood-stage prophylactic agents &mdash; mefloquine included &mdash;
    do not kill hypnozoites, so the relapse risk persists into the postpartum
    period. The only radical cure is an 8-aminoquinoline (primaquine or
    tafenoquine), but both are contraindicated in pregnancy for the same
    fetal-hemolysis reason. Management is therefore one of timing: defer
    radical cure until after delivery, then confirm a normal maternal G6PD and
    administer a 14-day primaquine course (or single-dose tafenoquine where the
    G6PD status is confidently normal and the patient is no longer pregnant) to
    eradicate the dormant liver forms and prevent future relapse.
  </p>
</div>
