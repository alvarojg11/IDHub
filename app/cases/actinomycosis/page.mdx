<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Actinomycosis is a chronic, slowly progressive bacterial infection caused by
    <em> Actinomyces </em> species and related genera. It often presents as an indurated,
    infiltrative, mass-like lesion and can be mistaken for malignancy.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Key clinical clues include contiguous spread across tissue planes, sinus-tract
    tendency, and tissue sulfur granules. Diagnosis is commonly delayed because routine
    cultures may be negative, especially after prior antibiotic exposure. When suspected,
    the microbiology laboratory should be alerted for anaerobic processing and prolonged incubation.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    For bulky or deep disease, treatment generally starts with high-dose IV penicillin-class
    therapy, often for 2–6 weeks, then transitions to a prolonged oral course (commonly
    total therapy 6–12 months, individualized by response and imaging). Source control,
    including image-guided drainage, is useful when collections are present.
  </p>
</div>

<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Teaching points
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <ul className="list-disc pl-6 text-lg leading-7 text-gray-800 space-y-2">
    <li>
      Actinomycosis can mimic malignancy because of chronic indurated mass-like lesions.
    </li>
    <li>
      Sulfur granules in tissue are a major diagnostic clue.
    </li>
    <li>
      Prior antibiotic exposure can reduce culture yield.
    </li>
    <li>
      Therapy is usually prolonged and should be tailored to disease burden and response.
    </li>
    <li>
      Drainage or limited procedures may support cure and reduce need for extensive surgery.
    </li>
  </ul>
</div>
