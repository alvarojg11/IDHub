<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    This case illustrates the importance of considering Chagas disease in patients from endemic regions presenting with cardiomyopathy. The clinical presentation combines classic features: 1) geographic origin (Colombia), 2) vector exposure history (kissing bug bites in childhood), 3) characteristic ECG findings (right bundle branch block with PVCs), and 4) echocardiographic findings (apical aneurysm with thrombus). Chagas cardiomyopathy typically presents 10-30 years after initial infection, explaining why this patient developed symptoms decades after immigration. The absence of traditional cardiovascular risk factors should raise suspicion for alternative etiologies.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The diagnostic approach prioritizes serological testing, as recommended by WHO guidelines. Two different serological methods are required due to variability in test performance. PCR has lower sensitivity in chronic disease but may be useful in specific situations. Endomyocardial biopsy is invasive and rarely indicated. Importantly, a positive serology confirms infection but does not prove causality of cardiomyopathy; clinical correlation is essential, as many infected individuals never develop cardiac disease.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Management focuses on comprehensive heart failure therapy with consideration of antiparasitic treatment. Evidence for benznidazole in established cardiomyopathy is limited (BENEFIT trial showed no improvement in cardiac outcomes), but it may be considered in select patients after shared decision-making. Cardiac complications of Chagas disease include heart failure, arrhythmias, thromboembolism (from apical thrombus), and sudden cardiac death. Apical aneurysm is pathognomonic and carries high thrombotic risk, often requiring anticoagulation.
  </p>
</div>
