<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Anti-GM-CSF autoantibodies are a recognized acquired immune defect linked to
    two major clinical phenotypes: pulmonary alveolar proteinosis (PAP) and
    <em> Cryptococcus gattii</em> cryptococcosis. In the Taiwanese multicenter cohort
    (Wang et al., 2022), 15 of 39 non-HIV cryptococcosis patients had high-titer
    neutralizing anti-GM-CSF autoantibodies.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    CNS involvement was prominent: 14 of 15 anti-GM-CSF-positive cryptococcosis
    patients had CNS disease, and most typed isolates in that subgroup were
    <em> C. gattii</em>. By contrast, <em>C. neoformans</em> predominated in the
    autoantibody-negative group.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    An important teaching point is phenotype separation. Despite sharing the same
    autoantibody risk factor, PAP and C. gattii cryptococcosis rarely overlapped
    in the same individual in the reported cohort and in population-level data.
    This suggests additional host/pathogen factors likely influence whether
    disease manifests primarily in lung surfactant homeostasis (PAP) or as
    invasive cryptococcosis.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Practical implication: when a patient with no classic immunocompromising
    condition presents with CNS cryptococcosis due to <em>C. gattii</em>, consider
    testing for anti-GM-CSF autoantibodies.
  </p>
</div>

<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Teaching points
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <ul className="list-disc pl-6 text-lg leading-7 text-gray-800 space-y-2">
    <li>
      In non-HIV patients with cryptococcosis, anti-GM-CSF autoantibodies are strongly associated with <em>C. gattii</em>, especially CNS disease.
    </li>
    <li>
      PAP and C. gattii cryptococcosis share anti-GM-CSF autoantibodies but usually do not present together in the same patient.
    </li>
    <li>
      Autoantibody titer and neutralizing activity alone may not fully explain why one patient develops PAP and another develops cryptococcosis.
    </li>
    <li>
      Screening for anti-GM-CSF autoantibodies is reasonable in otherwise immunocompetent patients with CNS <em>C. gattii</em> infection.
    </li>
  </ul>
</div>
