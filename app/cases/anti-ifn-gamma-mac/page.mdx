<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Disseminated MAC in an HIV-negative adult with a normal CD4 count, no immunosuppressive therapy, and no evidence of hematologic malignancy should immediately trigger a search for an alternative immune defect. The clinical presentation in this case, including the massive lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly, constitutional symptoms, hypermetabolic PET findings, and AFB-positive bone marrow, could be mistaken for high-grade lymphoma for weeks before cultures and biopsy return. The two indeterminate QuantiFERON-TB Gold results are not a laboratory failure: in anti-IFN-γ autoantibody syndrome, the neutralizing autoantibodies in patient plasma inactivate the IFN-γ released by T cells during the assay, causing both the TB antigen-specific and the mitogen stimulation wells to read as negative, producing an indeterminate result. Recognizing this as a diagnostic clue rather than a technical artifact accelerates the path to the correct diagnosis.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Anti-interferon-gamma autoantibody syndrome is an acquired immunodeficiency first described in detail in a landmark 2012 NEJM report by Browne and colleagues, who characterized 52 patients, predominantly from Thailand and Taiwan, with disseminated NTM and high titers of neutralizing anti-IFN-γ IgG4 autoantibodies. The syndrome exhibits striking geographic and ethnic clustering, with the strongest associations found in Southeast and East Asian populations, linked to specific HLA haplotypes (HLA-DRB1*16:02 and HLA-DQB1*05:02) that are thought to promote autoreactive T-cell help for the IFN-γ-specific B-cell response. Mechanistically, the autoantibodies block IFN-γ binding to its receptor on macrophages, abolishing macrophage activation, granuloma formation, and intracellular mycobacterial killing, producing a phenotype functionally identical to genetic MSMD. MAC is the most common organism, but the spectrum of pathogens also includes other NTM species, non-typhoidal Salmonella, <em>Talaromyces marneffei</em> in endemic regions, varicella-zoster virus, <em>Cryptococcus</em>, and <em>Histoplasma</em>.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Diagnosis is confirmed by detecting neutralizing anti-IFN-γ autoantibodies by ELISA, which measures the patient serum's capacity to block the biological activity of exogenous recombinant IFN-γ. High titers are characteristic. Whole exome sequencing would be indicated if a germline primary immunodeficiency were suspected, but the adult onset, Southeast Asian ethnicity, and lack of childhood infections point to the acquired syndrome. MAC treatment follows standard multidrug regimens: azithromycin or clarithromycin combined with ethambutol, with rifabutin commonly added for disseminated disease. Duration should be a minimum of 18 to 24 months, often longer, given the persistent immune defect; premature discontinuation frequently leads to relapse.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Addressing the autoantibody is essential for durable disease control. Rituximab depletes CD20-positive B cells and reduces autoantibody production, resulting in lower anti-IFN-γ titers and improved IFN-γ pathway function over weeks to months. Published case series support rituximab at 375 mg/m² weekly for four doses, with repeat courses used in patients who relapse immunologically. Cyclophosphamide has shown comparable efficacy in small comparative series and remains an alternative when rituximab is unavailable. Recombinant IFN-γ supplementation is neutralized by the circulating autoantibodies and has shown no consistent benefit. Plasmapheresis transiently lowers titers but provides no durable effect. In refractory cases, agents targeting plasma cells (daratumumab, an anti-CD38 antibody) have been reported with success in isolated cases.
  </p>
</div>
