<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    This is vertebral infection caused by the BCG strain of <em>Mycobacterium bovis</em> after intravesical therapy for non-muscle-invasive urothelial carcinoma. The presentation is classically delayed and indolent, often emerging many months after bladder instillation with progressive back pain, constitutional symptoms, elevated inflammatory markers, and destructive vertebral imaging findings. Because routine pyogenic cultures may be sterile and symptoms are often attributed to degenerative disease or malignancy, diagnosis is commonly delayed.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The key diagnostic step is obtaining tissue for pathology and mycobacterial studies. Growth of Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex does not by itself distinguish <em>Mycobacterium tuberculosis</em> from <em>Mycobacterium bovis</em>, but pyrazinamide resistance is a major clue because <em>M. bovis</em> is intrinsically resistant. A negative interferon-gamma release assay adds further support in this setting because BCG strains lack the RD1 antigens that drive those assays. The history of prior intravesical BCG, especially with traumatic catheterization or mucosal bleeding, provides the critical epidemiologic link.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Management differs from standard drug-susceptible tuberculosis because pyrazinamide should be omitted, as <em>Mycobacterium bovis</em> is usually intrinsically resistant to it. Most patients are treated with isoniazid, rifampin, and ethambutol for a prolonged course, often 6 to 12 months, adjusted to clinical, radiographic, and microbiologic response. Surgical consultation is important when there is spinal instability, cord compression, progressive neurologic deficit, or failure of medical therapy. Because BCG vertebral osteomyelitis can coexist with psoas abscess or mycotic aortic involvement, clinicians should keep a low threshold for broader imaging when pain is severe or anatomy suggests contiguous extension.
  </p>
</div>
