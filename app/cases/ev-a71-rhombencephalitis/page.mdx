<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Enterovirus A71 is a single-stranded positive-sense RNA virus of the
    Picornaviridae family and the most neurovirulent enterovirus since
    poliovirus eradication. It uses the scavenger receptor class B member 2
    (SCARB2) as its primary cellular receptor, which is expressed on neurons
    and epithelial cells, explaining its characteristic neurotropism. Since
    the landmark description of EV-A71 neurologic complications during the
    1998 Taiwan epidemic, rhombencephalitis has been recognized as the most
    feared complication, with a staging system that progresses from myoclonus
    and ataxia (stage 1), through cranial nerve involvement (stage 2), to
    autonomic instability and neurogenic pulmonary edema (stage 3).
    Mortality in stage 3 can exceed 70% without intervention, with death
    occurring within hours from fulminant neurogenic pulmonary edema driven
    by a catecholamine storm originating in the damaged brainstem.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Although EV-A71 rhombencephalitis is overwhelmingly a pediatric disease,
    adults can develop severe infection when protective immunity is impaired.
    The majority of reported adult cases occur in patients with B-cell
    depletion, most commonly from rituximab or after hematopoietic stem cell
    transplantation. Neutralizing antibodies are the primary mechanism of
    protection against EV-A71, and adults in endemic regions typically carry
    seroprevalence rates of 60 to 70% from prior childhood exposure. When
    B-cell function is abolished by rituximab, this humoral defense is lost,
    and even a previously immune adult can develop uncontrolled viremia and
    CNS invasion upon re-exposure, as likely occurred here through contact
    with his nephew. A key diagnostic pitfall in immunocompromised adults is
    that CSF pleocytosis may be absent entirely, reflecting the blunted
    inflammatory response rather than the severity of infection.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The MRI pattern of EV-A71 rhombencephalitis is highly characteristic.
    T2/FLAIR hyperintensity in the dorsal medulla oblongata and posterior
    pontine tegmentum reflects the segmental distribution of viral
    neurotropism along the brainstem, consistent with retrograde axonal
    transport from the oropharynx via cranial nerves. Bilateral dentate
    nucleus involvement is a distinctive feature that helps differentiate
    EV-A71 from other causes of rhombencephalitis. Dorsal medulla
    involvement is the single most powerful MRI predictor of poor outcome,
    with an accuracy of approximately 90% in published series. The absence
    of significant gadolinium enhancement is typical in the acute phase. MRI
    lesions in survivors often normalize over months, with approximately 84%
    showing complete resolution on long-term follow-up.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Multi-compartment PCR is the diagnostic standard because EV-A71 is shed
    from the gastrointestinal tract for weeks and from the oropharynx for one
    to two weeks, often at viral loads that exceed those found in CSF. CSF
    PCR alone misses 35 to 50% of confirmed cases. Testing throat swab,
    rectal swab, and CSF simultaneously maximizes yield and allows for
    speciation by VP1 gene sequencing. In immunocompromised patients, IgM
    serology is unreliable because B-cell depletion impairs the humoral
    response. Empiric ampicillin and acyclovir should be started for all
    rhombencephalitis until bacterial (particularly Listeria) and herpesvirus
    etiologies are excluded.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    No approved specific antiviral exists for EV-A71. Treatment is anchored
    on IVIG and aggressive supportive ICU care. IVIG provides passive
    neutralizing antibodies and modulates the inflammatory cascade through
    multiple mechanisms, including expansion of regulatory T-cell populations
    and suppression of IL-6, IL-8, and IL-10. In B-cell depleted patients,
    IVIG is the only means of supplying anti-EV-A71 neutralizing antibodies.
    Dosing is extrapolated from pediatric protocols at 1 to 2 g/kg. ICU
    management centers on early intubation for airway protection and
    respiratory failure, lung-protective ventilation for neurogenic pulmonary
    edema, hemodynamic support for autonomic storms, and close monitoring for
    the abrupt cardiovascular collapse that characterizes stage 3 disease.
    Milrinone has been studied in a single pediatric randomized trial in
    Vietnamese children with stage 3B disease and showed a mortality signal,
    but the evidence is limited and no adult data exists. Fluoxetine has
    demonstrated in vitro capsid inhibitor activity against enteroviruses and
    has been used adjunctively in published case reports alongside IVIG, but
    it remains investigational. Pleconaril, rupintrivir, and combination
    antiviral approaches are under investigation but not commercially
    available.
  </p>
</div>
