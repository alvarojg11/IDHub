<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    This case illustrates a classic nodular lymphangitic syndrome after traumatic
    inoculation from plant material. The board-relevant pattern is a primary papule or
    ulcer at the inoculation site followed by subcutaneous nodules extending proximally
    along lymphatic channels over days to weeks. That pattern should prompt a focused
    differential that includes sporotrichosis, primary cutaneous nocardiosis, and
    nontuberculous mycobacterial infection, especially <em>Mycobacterium marinum</em>.
    Exposure history helps narrow the field: thorn injury and gardening point strongly
    toward sporotrichosis.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The best next diagnostic step is lesion biopsy or aspirate for fungal culture and
    histopathology rather than repeat superficial swabs or serum fungal markers. Tissue
    burden may be low, so histopathology can be nondiagnostic, but culture is usually the
    key confirmatory test. <em>Sporothrix</em> species are dimorphic fungi, and clinicians
    should alert the laboratory when sporotrichosis is suspected so fungal culture is
    handled appropriately. The classic board description of cigar-shaped budding yeast is
    worth remembering, but those forms are often difficult to find in routine tissue
    sections.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    For uncomplicated lymphocutaneous disease, oral itraconazole is the preferred
    treatment. Severe pulmonary, disseminated, osteoarticular, or central nervous system
    disease may require initial amphotericin B followed by step-down azole therapy.
    Although saturated solution of potassium iodide remains historically associated with
    sporotrichosis, it is no longer the preferred first-line treatment in most modern
    practice settings. Lack of response to routine cellulitis antibiotics should prompt
    reconsideration of the diagnosis rather than repeated empiric antibacterial courses.
  </p>
</div>
