<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    <em>Mycobacterium bovis</em> is a member of the <em>M. tuberculosis</em> complex and can cause
    pulmonary, extrapulmonary, or disseminated tuberculosis in humans. In many modern cases in the
    United States, an important epidemiologic clue is ingestion of unpasteurized dairy products
    (such as queso fresco), particularly in patients with ties to regions where bovine TB exposure
    remains relevant.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The ileocecal region is a classic site of gastrointestinal tuberculosis, and chronic abdominal
    pain, diarrhea, weight loss, and abdominal lymphadenopathy can mimic inflammatory bowel disease
    or malignancy. This case also includes pulmonary imaging abnormalities, which broadens concern
    for TB-complex infection rather than an isolated gastrointestinal process.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    A major management implication of identifying <em>M. bovis</em> is intrinsic resistance to
    pyrazinamide (PZA). For that reason, standard 6-month therapy for drug-susceptible pulmonary
    <em>M. tuberculosis</em> (which depends on PZA to shorten duration) is not used. Treatment is
    typically extrapolated from pyrazinamide-resistant TB experience and guideline-based practice,
    commonly using isoniazid, rifampin, and ethambutol initially, followed by isoniazid plus
    rifampin to complete a longer course (often 9 months total in uncomplicated, drug-susceptible
    disease).
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Species-level identification within the <em>M. tuberculosis</em> complex may require genotypic
    methods (e.g., PCR-based methods, line probe assays, sequencing, or other laboratory workflows)
    in addition to culture growth. While precise laboratory workflows vary by institution, the
    clinical consequence is the same: recognizing <em>M. bovis</em> changes the regimen because PZA
    should be avoided.
  </p>
</div>
