<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Clinical Vignette
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    A 29-year-old man presents with 1 month of fever, drenching sweats, malaise,
    and reduced appetite. He reports daily temperatures at home, progressive fatigue,
    and an unintentional 4-kg weight loss. He has no known valvular disease and takes
    no chronic medications.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    He recently returned from visiting family in a rural area where he consumed
    unpasteurized goat cheese and helped care for sheep and goats. He denies injection
    drug use. On examination, he is febrile to 39.3 degrees C, tachycardic, and has splinter
    hemorrhages under several fingernails. Cardiac auscultation reveals a new diastolic
    murmur.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Laboratory studies show a white blood cell count of 8.9 x 10^9/L, hemoglobin
    12.6 g/dL, platelets 214 x 10^9/L, C-reactive protein 29 mg/dL, and erythrocyte
    sedimentation rate 58 mm/hour. Aspartate aminotransferase is 42 U/L and alanine
    aminotransferase is 39 U/L. Transthoracic echocardiography demonstrates a friable
    vegetation on the aortic valve with an aortic root abscess. Four blood culture sets
    remain negative at 48 hours, but one bottle later grows a small gram-negative
    coccobacillus after prolonged incubation. A standard tube agglutination test returns
    positive at a titer of 1:640.
  </p>
</div>

<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Brucellosis is a zoonotic infection acquired through direct contact with infected
    animals or by ingesting unpasteurized dairy products. <em>Brucella melitensis</em>,
    associated with sheep and goats, is the most invasive species in human disease and is
    a classic cause of severe brucellosis in endemic regions.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The organism is a small, nonmotile gram-negative coccobacillus and can be difficult
    to recover in routine culture. When brucellosis is suspected, prolonged incubation
    and laboratory notification matter because early cultures may appear negative. Standard
    agglutination testing remains a key diagnostic tool; a titer of at least 1:160 is
    generally supportive, and this patient&apos;s 1:640 result makes active infection highly
    likely in the correct clinical context.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Although cardiovascular involvement is uncommon, endocarditis is the most feared
    cardiac complication of brucellosis and accounts for most brucellosis-related deaths.
    The aortic valve is commonly involved, and complications may include root abscess,
    embolic stroke, heart failure, and conduction abnormalities. In the case series that
    informed this vignette, delayed blood culture positivity after 5 to 7 days helped
    establish the diagnosis when early cultures were unrevealing.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Treatment should be individualized, but complicated focal disease generally requires
    prolonged multidrug therapy rather than the 6-week regimens used for uncomplicated
    brucellosis. Common regimens include doxycycline plus rifampin with an aminoglycoside
    such as streptomycin or gentamicin; ceftriaxone may be added in selected CNS
    complications. Surgery should be strongly considered when there is heart failure,
    severe valvular dysfunction, uncontrolled infection, relapse, embolic complications,
    or abscess formation, all of which substantially increase risk.
  </p>
</div>

<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Teaching points
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <ul className="list-disc pl-6 text-lg leading-7 text-gray-800 space-y-2">
    <li>
      Brucellosis should be considered in prolonged fever with livestock exposure or
      unpasteurized dairy ingestion.
    </li>
    <li>
      Negative blood cultures at 48 hours do not exclude brucellosis; prolonged incubation
      can increase yield.
    </li>
    <li>
      Standard agglutination titers support diagnosis, but they must be interpreted with
      the clinical picture and exposure history.
    </li>
    <li>
      Endocarditis is uncommon in brucellosis but causes most brucellosis-related deaths.
    </li>
    <li>
      Root abscess or heart failure should prompt early cardiothoracic surgery involvement.
    </li>
  </ul>
</div>
