<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Influenza A is an enveloped, segmented, negative-sense RNA virus in the Orthomyxoviridae family. Its success as a respiratory pathogen starts with hemagglutinin, the surface glycoprotein that binds terminal sialic acid residues on airway epithelial glycoproteins and glycolipids. In human-adapted strains, binding to alpha-2,6-linked sialic acid in the upper airway is especially important. After endocytosis, low pH induces a conformational change in hemagglutinin that permits fusion of the viral envelope with the endosomal membrane, while the M2 ion channel acidifies the virion interior and allows uncoating. In immunocompromised hosts, impaired cellular immunity permits more extensive lower respiratory tract involvement, prolonged viral replication, and more severe pneumonia.
  </p>
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Oseltamivir works at a later stage of the viral life cycle. Influenza neuraminidase cleaves sialic acid residues from the host cell surface and from respiratory mucus so newly assembled virions can detach and spread to neighboring cells. Inhibiting neuraminidase effectively traps progeny virions at the epithelial surface and reduces onward infection of adjacent respiratory epithelium. This explains why neuraminidase inhibitors have the greatest effect when given early in illness, before extensive rounds of replication and tissue injury have already occurred. By contrast, baloxavir acts even earlier by inhibiting the polymerase acidic endonuclease required for cap-snatching, and adamantanes act at uncoating but are no longer useful because of widespread resistance.
  </p>
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Prevention is complicated by influenza's ability to keep changing. The viral RNA polymerase lacks proofreading fidelity, so point mutations accumulate gradually in the hemagglutinin and neuraminidase genes. That process, called antigenic drift, continuously remodels the antigenic surface recognized by neutralizing antibodies and drives the need for annual vaccine reformulation. Antigenic shift is different: it refers to abrupt reassortment of whole gene segments when two influenza A viruses co-infect the same host, and it is the mechanism behind pandemic strain emergence rather than routine seasonal escape.
  </p>
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    For patients like this one, prevention is layered rather than singular. Annual inactivated influenza vaccination remains essential for the patient, household contacts, and health care workers. Because influenza is transmitted predominantly by large respiratory droplets and close-range exposure, droplet precautions and source control are important in the hospital. In high-risk exposures or institutional outbreaks, neuraminidase inhibitor chemoprophylaxis can be used for selected close contacts. The reason this is necessary in transplant recipients is not just exposure intensity; it is the combination of viral evolution, severe host vulnerability, and the potential for prolonged shedding in immunocompromised hosts.
  </p>
</div>
