The diagnostic approach begins with recognizing the triad of high fever, severe symmetric polyarthralgia, and maculopapular rash. After day 7, IgM serology becomes the primary diagnostic method, though false negatives may occur early. Testing should include dengue virus due to overlapping epidemiology and clinical features. Public health reporting is mandatory in most jurisdictions as chikungunya is a nationally notifiable disease. According to CDC guidelines, travelers with suspected chikungunya should be reported to local health departments to facilitate surveillance and outbreak detection.

    Management is supportive with antipyretics and NSAIDs for arthritis. Aspirin should be avoided due to bleeding risk, especially with thrombocytopenia. Most patients recover within 1-2 weeks, but approximately 30-40% develop chronic arthralgia lasting months to years. Risk factors for persistent arthritis include older age, high initial viral load, and pre-existing rheumatological disease. Serious complications are rare but include encephalitis, myocarditis, and Guillain-Barré syndrome.     Prevention focuses on mosquito avoidance (DEET, permethrin-treated clothing, screened accommodations) and elimination of breeding sites. A virus-like particle vaccine (VIMKUNYA) is approved in the United States for individuals aged 12 years and older.
