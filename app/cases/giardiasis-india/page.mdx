<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    This patient has a classic small-bowel diarrheal syndrome: relatively bulky loose
    stools, weight loss, flatulence, and malabsorptive features rather than dysentery.
    The evolution from watery stools to greasy, foul-smelling stools and the worsening
    after milk products point toward proximal intestinal dysfunction rather than invasive
    colitis.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The main epidemiologic clue is exposure to untreated water during backpacking.
    <em>Giardia intestinalis</em> (also called <em>G. lamblia</em>) is a noninvasive
    flagellated protozoan that colonizes the small intestine and commonly affects
    travelers exposed to contaminated water or food in endemic settings. In this case,
    stool microscopy later confirms the diagnosis by showing trophozoites on fresh
    examination and cysts on concentrated stool testing.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Positive stool reducing substances support secondary carbohydrate malabsorption,
    most likely transient lactose intolerance after enteric infection. Giardia can
    impair intestinal absorption and produce both carbohydrate intolerance and fat
    malabsorption, explaining bloating after dairy and greasy stools.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Tinidazole 2 g orally once is an appropriate treatment for uncomplicated giardiasis.
    Metronidazole is another standard option, but requires a multi-day course. Temporary
    restriction of milk and other high-sugar foods can reduce symptoms while the
    post-infectious malabsorption resolves over the next several days to weeks.
  </p>
</div>

<CaseReveal pollId="case-giardiasis-india-q2">
<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Teaching points
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <ul className="list-disc pl-6 text-lg leading-7 text-gray-800 space-y-2">
    <li>
      Clinical features can help localize infectious diarrhea when laboratory support is limited:
      high-volume watery or bulky stools suggest small-bowel disease, whereas frequent small-volume
      stools, urgency, tenesmus, or blood suggest colonic inflammation.
    </li>
    <li>
      Persistent diarrhea lasting more than 2 weeks with flatulence, greasy stools, weight loss,
      and bloating after dairy is a strong clue for giardiasis and secondary malabsorption.
    </li>
    <li>
      <em>Giardia</em> commonly causes carbohydrate intolerance and fat malabsorption; positive stool
      reducing substances support this complication.
    </li>
    <li>
      Important post-enteric complications to remember include hemolytic uremic syndrome after
      enterohemorrhagic <em>E. coli</em> or <em>Shigella dysenteriae</em>, Guillain-Barre syndrome
      after <em>Campylobacter jejuni</em>, reactive arthritis after <em>Campylobacter</em>,
      <em>Salmonella</em>, <em>Shigella</em>, or <em>Yersinia</em>, and liver abscess from
      <em>Entamoeba histolytica</em>.
    </li>
    <li>
      Nitroimidazoles remain standard treatment for giardiasis, with tinidazole often preferred
      because it is effective as a single 2 g oral dose.
    </li>
    <li>
      Travelers should be counseled about food and water safety, especially avoidance of unsafe
      drinking water and foods prepared under poor hygienic conditions.
    </li>
  </ul>
</div>
