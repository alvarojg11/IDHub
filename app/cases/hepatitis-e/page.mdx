<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The correct diagnosis requires reasoning through the differential before viral PCR results are available. CMV, EBV, and HSV hepatitis are all legitimate considerations in a transplant recipient with unexplained LFT elevation, but each has features that reduce its probability here: CMV hepatitis typically causes fever, lymphocytosis, and often other end-organ disease; HSV hepatitis causes fulminant transaminase elevation far exceeding the mild elevations seen here; and EBV-driven PTLD is most common in the first two years after transplantation and in the absence of lymphadenopathy or lymphocytosis. The normal tacrolimus level excludes drug toxicity as the explanation. What remains, and what the exposure history points to directly, is hepatitis E virus genotype 3: a zoonotic pathogen transmitted through undercooked pork, venison, and wild game, endemic in the US and Europe without any requirement for international travel. The medium-rare venison consumed eight weeks before presentation is the operative exposure.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Hepatitis E virus is a positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus in the family Hepeviridae. Genotypes 1 and 2 are transmitted via fecal-oral contamination of water in low-income countries and affect immunocompetent hosts; genotype 1 is particularly severe in pregnant women, with maternal mortality approaching 25% in the third trimester due to the combined effects of altered Th2 immune polarization, estrogen-driven viral replication, and disordered placental function. Genotypes 1 and 2 do not cause chronic infection. Genotypes 3 and 4 are zoonotic; pigs, wild boar, and deer are the primary reservoirs in developed countries. In immunocompetent individuals, GT3 causes self-limited acute hepatitis. In solid organ transplant recipients and others with deficient cellular immunity, including persons with HIV and hematologic malignancies, GT3 fails to clear and establishes chronic infection, defined by persistent HEV RNA beyond three months. Tacrolimus has been specifically implicated as a risk factor for chronification compared to cyclosporine, possibly through selective impairment of T-cell-mediated viral clearance.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    The diagnostic approach in immunosuppressed patients must account for the unreliability of serology. Anti-HEV IgM and IgG depend on intact humoral immunity that is specifically suppressed by the transplant immunosuppression regimen. In published series of solid organ transplant recipients with confirmed HEV infection, a substantial proportion are seronegative at the time of diagnosis. HEV RNA in blood detected by real-time PCR is the required test and is also the instrument of monitoring during and after treatment. Stool HEV RNA can complement blood testing, as fecal shedding may precede or outlast viremia.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Treatment follows a stepwise protocol. Reduction of immunosuppression is the first intervention, achieving viral clearance in approximately 30% of patients without the need for antivirals. If HEV RNA remains detectable after three months, ribavirin is started at approximately 600 mg/day (mean 8.6 mg/kg/day in the largest multicenter study), adjusted downward for renal function and baseline hemoglobin. A three-month first course achieves sustained virologic response in 78 to 81% of patients; a second course raises cumulative SVR to approximately 90% in relapsers. Hemolytic anemia is the dominant toxicity and erythropoietin is needed in approximately 40% of treated patients. Sofosbuvir add-on therapy has been specifically studied and shown not to improve outcomes. Pegylated interferon, which carries a rejection risk and is contraindicated in liver transplant recipients, is reserved for ribavirin failure in selected kidney transplant recipients after individualized risk-benefit assessment.
  </p>
</div>
