<CaseReveal pollId="case-paragonimiasis-q1">
<h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mb-6">
  Rationale
</h2>

<div className="mx-auto max-w-5xl">
  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Paragonimiasis is a food-borne trematode infection acquired when metacercariae
    are ingested in raw or undercooked freshwater crabs or crayfish. After
    ingestion, larvae migrate through tissue and ultimately localize in the lungs,
    where chronic inflammatory cystic lesions can form.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Pulmonary disease typically presents with chronic cough and episodic
    hemoptysis and may mimic pulmonary tuberculosis radiographically, including
    nodules, infiltrates, and cavitary lesions. Peripheral eosinophilia and
    compatible exposure history are high-yield clues.
  </p>

  <p className="text-lg leading-6 text-gray-800 mb-6 text-justify">
    Definitive diagnosis is made by identifying Paragonimus eggs in sputum, stool,
    or pleural specimens; serology (including ELISA-based testing) can support
    diagnosis, especially in extrapulmonary disease.
  </p>
</div>
