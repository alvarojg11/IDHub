This LPS layer is hydrophobic and restricts the entry of many antibiotics. To permit access for hydrophilic nutrients and metabolites, these organisms express porins&#8212;protein channels that allow small, water-soluble molecules to diffuse through the outer membrane.</p><p>In <em>Escherichia coli</em>, for instance, the major porins OmpF and OmpC are differentially expressed depending on environmental osmolarity: OmpF predominates in low-osmolarity environments, while OmpC is upregulated under hyperosmolar conditions. In general, small, hydrophilic molecules such as imipenem diffuse readily, whereas large, hydrophobic, or negatively charged agents do not.</p><p>Mutations that reduce the number or function of porins markedly diminish permeability. The <strong>RND (resistance-nodulation-cell division)</strong> family of pumps exports fluoroquinolones, tetracyclines, aminoglycosides, and most &#946;-lactams.
